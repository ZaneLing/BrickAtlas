\begin{abstract}
Brick-assembly evaluation has advanced in generation, spatial reasoning,
and final-state validity, but a valid final object does not certify a
constructible, recoverable, or functional process. We introduce
BrickAtlas Mechanism-1, a diagnostic benchmark built around six original
articulated objects and eight task families: prefix dynamics, continuous
service access, causal fault recovery, finite-inventory substitution,
dynamic impulse robustness, functional kinematics, active inspection,
and multi-objective design. Unlike our retired random-growth layer,
all publication-facing objects are coherent systems with wheels, axles,
gears, hinges, sliding carriages, payloads, and service bays.
Rapier 3D supplies deterministic rigid-body simulation, fixed/revolute/
prismatic joints, motor actuation, and swept-volume shape casts.
The release contains 48 paired tasks, 72 audited renders, complete
machine-readable inputs and oracles, and a per-case browser.
Under the same one-image minimal harness, GPT-4.1 mini solves 28/48
cases and Gemini 2.5 Flash 31/48; a local Qwen3-0.6B text baseline solves
3/48. Prefix and finite-stock questions are substantially easier than
continuous access, exact repair, functional outcomes, and complete
Pareto sets. The six public sources are development data, and simulator
evidence is not a calibrated claim about LEGO clutch force or robot
execution.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Recent brick systems generate plausible objects, infer assembly steps,
or answer spatial questions. Yet many evaluations still terminate at
a final structure or a single next action. Real construction requires
more: every prefix must survive gravity, a part or tool must reach its
target, failures must be diagnosed and repaired locally, mechanisms
must move as intended, and an agent must ask for information when the
state is ambiguous.

These requirements are coupled. A visually convincing crane can have
an inaccessible trolley; a correct drawbridge geometry can have a
reversed hinge; a robust plan can become invalid after one missing
brace; and a cheap replacement can violate stiffness requirements.
Aggregating these failures into final IoU, validity, or a language-model
judge hides what a system actually accomplished.

BrickAtlas Mechanism-1 asks whether a model can reason about
\emph{constructible processes} on shared, functional objects.
Figure~\ref{fig:teaser} shows the complete source set and
Fig.~\ref{fig:tasks} the eight evaluation families. We intentionally
do not import OMR geometry as cases: those models are retained only as
design-complexity references. The previous 5,120 random-growth objects
are likewise retired to regression testing.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item six original articulated brick-system designs with explicit rigid
modules, collision geometry, joints, service interfaces, inventories,
and hidden-state alternatives;
\item eight gap-driven tasks with deterministic physics, collision,
optimization, and information-gain oracles;
\item a fully inspectable case browser exposing every question, capability,
input, oracle, and model answer; and
\item a paired development study of two VLMs and one local text model,
with raw outputs, costs, and source-grouped reporting.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Generation and final validity.}
BrickGPT~\cite{pun2025brickgpt} and BrickNet~\cite{kulits2026bricknet}
generate brick structures using physical checks or typed connection
graphs. BrECS~\cite{ahn2024brecs} studies budget-aware sequential
assembly. These works establish that stable generation and resource
constraints are active research areas; our target is not another
text-to-brick generator, but process-level diagnosis after a design
exists.

\paragraph{Visual and procedural reasoning.}
LEGO-Puzzles~\cite{tang2025legopuzzles} and
PhyBlock~\cite{ma2025phyblock} cover spatial, ordering, diagnostic, and
counterfactual questions. MEPNet~\cite{wang2022manual}, Neural
Assembler~\cite{yan2024assembler}, and TreeSBA~\cite{guo2024treesba}
infer poses or sequences from manuals and views. Co-builder
\cite{huang2025cobuilder} studies assembly-state detection.
Mechanism-1 complements these benchmarks with continuous shape casts,
dynamic thresholds, explicit joints, minimal recovery, and costed
clarification.

\paragraph{Interactive and embodied assembly.}
Break and Make~\cite{walsman2022break} uses interactive disassembly and
reconstruction. WorkBenchMark~\cite{ma2026workbenchmark} includes
robot/VLA execution, while BC-Bench~\cite{liu2026composer} evaluates
selection and pose. We do not claim robot realism. Rapier provides
deterministic digital rigid-body evidence; grasp, tolerance, clutch
force, and hardware transfer remain separate questions.

benchmark and combines visually coherent subassemblies rather than

A visible part has shape, size, local pose, material color, and module
membership. The vocabulary includes bricks, plates, beams, panels,
slopes, arches, windows, cylinders, wheels, axles, gears, and spheres.
A module has a world pose, mass, role, and anchored/dynamic state.
Joints are fixed, revolute, prismatic, or spring descriptors with local
anchors, axes, limits, and optional actuation.

OMR/LDraw examples are not included in the task manifest, source groups,
scores, or model inputs. They only guided an internal design review of
object coherence and subassembly granularity.

\subsection{Physics and Collision Oracle}
We use Rapier 3D v0.20 at 120\,Hz, 12 solver iterations, and two internal
PGS iterations. Compound colliders approximate the visual parts; slopes
and arches use conservative cuboid envelopes. Prefix evaluation rebuilds
each partial assembly, enables available joints, runs one simulated
second, and records the first module displacement above 0.35 units.

Accessibility uses a continuous cuboid shape cast from start pose to
service interface. Dynamic robustness applies an off-center impulse and
measures
\begin{equation}
d_{\max}=\max_t\max(\|\mathbf{x}_t-\mathbf{x}_0\|,
r\theta_t),
\end{equation}
where $r$ is the target module radius and $\theta_t$ its quaternion
rotation from the settled pose. Functional tasks motor a named joint
and compare measured motion with a declared fraction of the target.
All thresholds and engine settings are public and frozen.

\paragraph{Prefix dynamics and access.}
These tasks operationalize two widely noted gaps: final validity does
not imply stable prefixes, and a target pose does not imply an executable
insertion. The candidate order is not silently repaired. Shape casts
return every feasible path rather than one private reference.

\paragraph{Recovery and resources.}
Fault cases remove one structural or service joint, expose the simulated
child displacement and tilt, and require a complete minimum repair
program. Inventory cases force selection among a costly stiff beam,
paired trusses, a light cable, and an ineffective fairing under explicit
piece, cost, and stiffness limits.

\paragraph{Dynamics and function.}
Robustness is a threshold task over measured impulses, not a static
``stable'' label. Functional cases deliberately include successful and
failed actuation outcomes caused by load, joint limits, or interference.
Thus appearance alone cannot solve the task.

\paragraph{Inspection and engineering trade-offs.}
Active inspection scores expected information gain per unit cost over
four hidden worlds: nominal, reversed, missing-brace, and jammed.
Query costs vary by source, preventing one fixed query from succeeding.
The design task requires the complete Pareto set under lower cost,
lower mass, and higher stiffness.

\section{Evaluation Protocol}
Each task supplies one rendered view and structured evidence under a
fixed minimal harness. Models return one JSON object; unknown fields do
not repair missing required values. Missing, malformed, truncated, and
duplicate submissions remain in the denominator. Exact success is
reported separately for all eight families, with the source object as
the statistical cluster.

The deterministic tracks are:
\textbf{physics oracle}, which executes Rapier dynamics or shape casts;
\textbf{constraint oracle}, which enumerates inventory/Pareto answers;
and \textbf{information oracle}, which computes finite-world query
utility. Model outputs never receive oracle feedback.

We test GPT-4.1 mini and Gemini 2.5 Flash with the same image, structured
input, temperature zero, 2,200-token cap, no retry, and no provider
fallback. Qwen3-0.6B is a local text-only lower baseline. All 96 paid
calls cost \$0.069427; cumulative project spend remains below the
pre-existing \$4.50 cap.

GPT-4.1 mini solves 28/48 cases, Gemini 31/48, and local Qwen3-0.6B
3/48. Both VLMs solve all finite-inventory cases, while prefix accuracy
is 5/6 and 6/6. This does not make those families redundant: the valid order and numeric limits
are explicitly represented, and the six-source scale is small.

The largest gaps are more diagnostic. GPT solves 3/6 access cases and
no exact repair case; Gemini solves 3/6 access and 3/6 repair. Functional
outcomes are 3/6 for both because some actuators fail in simulation
despite valid-looking geometry. GPT obtains 5/6 dynamic thresholds while
Gemini obtains 2/6. Complete Pareto sets remain difficult (2/6 and 4/6).
These failures persist despite perfect output formatting, so they are not
explained by JSON parsing.

The task families also avoid one common answer template. Feasible path
sets vary across at least four patterns; safe impulse thresholds vary
across at least four values; active-inspection optima cover marker,
brace, and probe queries; and functional labels include both outcomes.
The local model's 3/48 result further rejects a broad format-only shortcut.

\section{Limitations}
Mechanism-1 contains only six public authored sources. The objects were
used during task design, so they are development data rather than an
independent confirmation set. Visual parts are LEGO-like primitives,
not a claim of compatibility with every commercial element.

Rapier validates rigid-body, joint, motor, and collision consequences
under declared parameters. It does not calibrate ABS elasticity, stud
clutch, manufacturing tolerance, wear, gripper contact, or human
ergonomics. Slope and arch physics use conservative collision envelopes.
Real-brick and robot transfer require separate experiments.

The model comparison is descriptive: six source clusters cannot support
fine-grained ranking or broad population claims. Human difficulty,
independent object design, additional model families, multi-seed
sampling, and real hardware remain outstanding. The complete browser
and Chinese casebook expose every task and answer so these limitations
can be audited rather than hidden behind an aggregate score.

\section{Conclusion}
BrickAtlas Mechanism shifts brick evaluation from final appearance and
axis-aligned validity to process, intervention, and function. Original
articulated objects, external rigid-body simulation, continuous shape
casts, exact repair programs, active inspection, and Pareto scoring
produce failures that earlier recoloring and height-sorting tasks could
not reveal. The first model study confirms substantial headroom in
accessibility, recovery, functional prediction, dynamic thresholds, and
multi-objective reasoning, while preserving explicit boundaries between
simulation evidence and real-world mechanics.
