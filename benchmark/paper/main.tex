\begin{abstract}
An assembly benchmark needs identifiable parts and trustworthy geometry
before its reasoning scores can be interpreted. We present BrickAtlas
LDraw-1, a replacement dataset built from \LDrawSources{} unmodified
LDraw/OMR source models, containing \LDrawParts{} part instances and
\LDrawTasks{} questions. Each source instance receives a stable number
shared by the interactive renderer, public visual inputs, task operands,
scorer, and human-review records. Questions use source attributes,
author STEP metadata, recognized connector graphs, or explicitly scoped
scene edits. A connector and inset-mesh audit reports its coverage and
unresolved intersection candidates without asserting physical stability.
Source reparsing reproduces every retained instance identity, color and
pose; all reference answers pass their contracts. These are software
controls, not measurements of model reasoning. We provide the original
source locks, exclusions, pairwise similarity screens, numbered inputs,
four-view appendix, and a review workflow. The release retires the
previous synthetic geometry and its physical conclusions; model
performance and human difficulty remain unmeasured.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Visual plausibility is an insufficient foundation for evaluating
assembly reasoning. A row of blocks may resemble a mechanism while
having no real mating interfaces. Several near-identical layouts may
inflate a dataset's size without adding structural variety. A prompt
that names a ``support'' may be impossible to ground when several
pieces share that description. Each issue can invalidate an apparently
precise score before the model makes a reasoning error.

These failures motivated a complete replacement of the earlier
BrickAtlas Hierarchy-3 release. Human inspection identified disconnected
imitation geometry, repeated construction patterns, and ambiguous
module references. Its nominal servo checks did not establish real LEGO
connectivity. We therefore retire that release as the active benchmark
and do not transfer its counts, physical passes, or pilot model scores
to LDraw-1.

The replacement fixes the source object and authors questions around
the evidence actually available. We retain complete original OMR files,
their authors and licenses, and their dependency closures. Every physical
part instance in the source has a stable \texttt{B0001}-style number.
The number identifies an instance, not a reusable part type: two
identical bricks receive different numbers. Relevant numbers appear
with leader lines in the 3D view and in exported visual inputs.
An instance can also be isolated, rotated, and inspected at its source
pose.

The task contract separates four kinds of evidence. Visual questions
require a numbered rendering. Source-data questions supply the relevant
attributes or instruction records. Graph questions reason about
recognized connector edges. Scene-edit tasks reveal an original instance
or replay an author's steps. None of these contracts establishes forces,
friction, stability, or an insertion trajectory.

Our contributions are a source-faithful replacement corpus, a common
numbering interface for observation and evaluation, and reproducible
checks that expose the limits of structural evidence. We release a
reviewable dataset and protocol rather than a model leaderboard.

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Interactive and generative assembly.}
Break and Make~\cite{walsman2022break} studies interactive structural
understanding through disassembly and reconstruction.
Visual manual translation~\cite{wang2022manual} connects assembly
instructions with executable plans.
BrickNet~\cite{kulits2026bricknet} provides a graph-backed representation
of brick interfaces, while BrickGPT~\cite{pun2025brickgpt} studies
physically stable and buildable generation. LDraw-1 uses the public
BrickNet connector implementation as an audit tool for fixed original
sources. It does not reproduce the generative or robotic evaluation
protocols of those works.

\paragraph{Visual and physical reasoning.}
CLEVR~\cite{johnson2017clevr} and GQA~\cite{hudson2019gqa} motivate
explicitly compositional questions with inspectable answers.
BLINK~\cite{fu2024blink} highlights limitations in basic visual
perception. LEGO-Puzzles~\cite{tang2025legopuzzles} and
PhyBlock~\cite{ma2025phyblock} evaluate spatial and physical planning
under their respective observation and simulation contracts.
Our contribution concerns source authenticity, instance grounding,
and the boundary between CAD evidence and physical claims.

\section{Source Corpus}
\subsection{Acquisition and Identity}
LDraw represents a reference by a part name, color, translation and
linear transform~\cite{ldraw}. An MPD file may contain nested submodels
and custom parts. We resolve the complete dependency closure and flatten
the source hierarchy into part instances while retaining each instance's
path, source file and line. Three.js LDrawLoader produces the real part
meshes~\cite{threejs}. A single documented conversion maps LDraw units
and axes to the viewer's millimeter coordinate frame.

We preserve the original MPD bytes and their SHA256 digest. The website
provides the unchanged source file, author, license, and source URL.
The stored scene transform, color and part type are compared with a fresh
source parse for every instance. Mesh chunks and dependencies have
separate checksums. Grouping for display has no role in the connector
ground truth.

\subsection{Selection and Exclusions}
The preparation inventory contains 43 candidates from the existing
source catalog and newly acquired OMR files. Of these, 29 complete
sources were converted. Fourteen were excluded during preparation
for explicit reasons such as unresolved or unsupported geometry,
attribution gaps, oversized models, singular transforms, or a mismatch
between source instances and loader identities. Five additional sources
were excluded after structural auditing: duplicate placements or
connector-definition coverage below 90\%. The retained collection
contains \LDrawSources{} sources from \LDrawSources{} set families.
The machine-readable release manifest preserves every exclusion reason.

The 90\% threshold limits the amount of unsupported input to graph
questions. It is not a connectivity or quality score. Unknown parts
remain in the displayed source and are reported explicitly; they are
never deleted to raise coverage. A source scene can legitimately contain
separate objects, such as a vehicle and a figure. We do not invent edges
between them.

\subsection{Diversity Screens}
Only one primary build is retained per set family. Alternative builds,
recolors and generated variants do not increase the source count.
We compute a geometry fingerprint after removing source labels, colors,
instance ordering and global translation. The retained sources have
distinct fingerprints.

We additionally report all 276 source-pair comparisons. Inventory
similarity is a multiset Jaccard coefficient,
\begin{equation}
J(A,B)=
\frac{\sum_p\min(c_A(p),c_B(p))}
{\sum_p\max(c_A(p),c_B(p))},
\end{equation}
where $c_A(p)$ is the number of instances of type $p$ in source $A$.
The maximum observed value is 0.450, between the large tractor and jet.
Shared Technic inventories can produce this overlap even for distinct
objects. A centered, scale-normalized nearest-center shape score is
also supplied as a candidate screen. It is sensitive to orientation,
sampling density and shared outlines; neither screen proves statistical
independence. Human review remains necessary.

\section{Structural Evidence}
\subsection{Connector Matching}
We apply BrickNet 0.1.0 connector matching to the unchanged source
placements, including its alias definitions and upstream tolerances.
The algorithm recognizes stud/hole, fixed, axle, hinge and ball interface
families. A graph vertex is a numbered source instance; an edge records
the two connector indices, recognized family, and associated orientation
or slide parameters. Multiple connector records can link the same pair.
Graph questions use an undirected simple adjacency relation.

Coverage measures whether a part type has a connector definition.
Connected components describe the graph that the matcher recognized.
Neither quantity is the probability that the real assembly is connected.
Custom flexible pieces, decorative parts and some printed variants
can lack definitions. Alias matching and tolerances can also miss or
overstate a contact.

The upstream parser normalizes rotation matrices during connector
inference. We record its maximum matrix deviation per source. The
original source matrices remain the rendering ground truth. Inset-mesh
collision queries use the original alias-transformed matrices, not
the normalized graph poses.

\subsection{Intersection Candidates}
Inset collision meshes provide pairwise intersection candidates.
Intersections at friction pins, gears and other mating geometry are
not automatically defects. We report both all detected intersections
and those without a recognized mating interface. The latter total
\LDrawIntersections{} pairs across retained sources and remain visible
in the review evidence. We neither suppress them nor move source
parts to remove them.

This audit does not measure forces, masses, friction, material deformation
or passive dynamics. An accepted source is an authentic, reproducibly
imported CAD object, not a certified physical assembly. Resolving the
remaining candidates requires connector-aware inspection, more complete
geometry definitions, or physical validation.

\section{Numbered Tasks and Observations}
\subsection{Instance Grounding}
Let $M=\{(b_i,p_i,c_i,T_i)\}_{i=1}^n$ denote the source instances:
$b_i$ is a stable instance ID, $p_i$ a part type, $c_i$ a color, and
$T_i$ the original pose. The displayed number
$\ell_i=\texttt{B}\Vert\mathrm{pad}_4(i)$ maps to exactly one $b_i$
within a source hash. A globally unambiguous reference is therefore
$(\mathrm{sourceHash},b_i)$.

A question binds every operand to both $b_i$ and $\ell_i$.
Leader lines anchor numbers at projected geometry centers. A label
is an identity pointer, not proof that the part is unoccluded.
The interface provides individual isolation and numbered inventory
search. Dense tasks show at most twelve labels simultaneously, and every
operand can be inspected separately. Screenshot export composites the
same overlay with the model canvas.

\subsection{Task Applicability}
The release contains eleven families (Table~\ref{tab:families}).
Tasks are generated only when their required evidence exists.
Visual type matching requires another instance of the same source
part type and different-type distractors. Source-step tasks require
author STEP provenance. An editorial grouping or file-list order never
substitutes for author instructions.

Visual tasks include color identification and part-type correspondence.
Their public records omit part names, color attributes and solutions.
Each of the \LDrawVisual{} visual questions has a numbered operand image,
and interactive evaluation can rotate, zoom or isolate the source.
Coordinate questions explicitly supply geometry bounding-box centers;
these are not centers of mass.

Connector questions classify supplied records, identify all direct
neighbors, and count components after deleting a numbered vertex from
an induced local graph. Deletion is a graph operation. It does not mean
that the corresponding physical brick can be extracted without moving
its neighbors. Coverage and evidence-limit questions ask what can
actually be concluded from a partial audit.

\subsection{Source Steps and Scene Edits}
Seventeen retained sources provide usable author STEP records.
The viewer replays their cumulative visible instances at the original
poses. It does not interpolate fictional insertion paths.
Sources without usable author steps have no replay or source-order
questions.

The restoration family temporarily hides a numbered instance in a
display copy. A correct action restores that instance at its original
pose and preserves the remaining visibility. This is an editor task
with a precise identity target. The source-sequence family reveals a
short author-step window in order. It evaluates adherence to given
source instructions, not a newly inferred mechanical assembly order.

\section{Scoring and Review}
Choice tasks use exact option IDs; set-valued outputs require the exact
set with no duplicate IDs. Operation tasks use a public transition
system. Each action has required facts, forbidden facts, additions,
deletions and cost. The interpreter rejects unknown actions, violated
preconditions, repeated completed steps and budget overruns. Success
requires all goal facts and no prohibited facts.

The same interpreter drives the website's operation replay. A rejected
operation cannot silently update the visual state. Reference solutions
are available in explicitly labeled review artifacts; model-facing JSON
excludes them. Because this is a public development corpus, the review
assets are not a secure hidden test set. Evaluation harnesses must
provide only the designated inputs and log tool access.

Reviewers mark each task pass or fail. Failure requires a reason.
Records include source hash, task ID, numbered operands, decision and
timestamp. Browser storage uses a new LDraw-1 namespace. Export and
import support batch corrections without overwriting historical human
feedback. Approval is a reviewer judgment; no automatic validation step
writes a human pass.

Independent source reparsing reproduces \LDrawParts{} instance records
across all \LDrawSources{} sources. Checks cover identity, hierarchy,
part type, color, transforms, dependency closure and mesh checksums.
All \LDrawTasks{} reference answers are accepted. A public-precondition
solver satisfies all 75 scene-edit tasks. A total of 1,606 invalid
choices or action sequences are rejected. Number mappings and visual
input schemas are checked separately.

All 24 sources load their full instance count in the browser. Canonical
perspective, front, side and top renders and numbered example views
are captured from the same application. Browser tests exercise instance
isolation, explosion, restoration, author-step playback, review
persistence, JSON round trips and mobile controls.

These controls are necessary for reproducibility but do not establish
dataset difficulty or real-world validity. We have not run a new
language-model or vision-language-model evaluation on LDraw-1, and make
no comparative model-performance claim. The earlier synthetic pilot
is excluded entirely.

\paragraph{Proposed evaluation.}
A future frozen evaluation should separate image-only, interactive
visual, source-data and graph inputs. It should report exact success,
format validity, tool usage, and source-macro averages. Splits must
respect set families and inspect high-similarity source pairs.
Human audits should report agreement and error categories.
Instance-number permutation and option-order controls can detect
shortcuts. These experiments remain proposed, with no filled-in model
accuracy cells.

\section{Limitations and Release Status}
The corpus is small and has uneven source scales. Several task families
are deliberately elementary evidence controls. Parts within one source
are not independent samples, and common LEGO inventories may span
sources. Familiar commercial sets may occur in model pretraining.
Public reference artifacts make contamination possible.

Original OMR geometry is stronger evidence than imitation blocks, but
it is still CAD authored by contributors. Unsupported connectors,
intersection candidates and source conventions require review.
Our 90\% coverage threshold is an operational selection rule, not an
empirically calibrated physical threshold. The source-step order may
differ from official instruction-book pages or a feasible robot plan.

LDraw-1 is therefore a source-faithful review release. Its current
contribution is a verifiable object-and-question interface with explicit
limits. Claims about stability, forces, coupled motion, insertion or
extraction will require additional evidence before becoming benchmark
ground truth.
