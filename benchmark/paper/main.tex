\begin{abstract}
Brick-structure reasoning combines recognition, spatial relations,
reconstruction, generation, editing, and executable planning. These tasks
require different inputs and notions of correctness: a plausible hidden
layout need not match one reference, while a submitted assembly order must
actually execute. We present the current development release of
BrickAtlas, a versioned casebank with 5,120 distinct procedural geometries,
117,760 object-derived task-condition cases, and 150 separate known-part
questions. Each case includes a machine-readable ground-truth record,
an independently implemented integer-cell geometry certificate, applicable
metrics, and replayable evaluation evidence. We separate ordinary RGB,
layer disclosure, and privileged symbolic references, and distinguish
model submissions from harness operations and evaluator diagnostics.
On the complete casebank, a public-input algorithm solves symbolic
relations and edits, but greedy assembly succeeds on only 2,364 of 5,120
structures, despite complete disassembly success. This demonstrates a
concrete distinction between current-action legality and future
assemblability, not a neural-model ranking. We also report, separately,
small neural pilots on earlier dataset versions. All 117,910 v2 cases
pass oracle and empty-answer audits and are independently regenerated
for artifact verification. The release is an executable research
resource, not yet a confirmatory benchmark study: independent human
review, broad multimodal baselines, semantic diversity, and
token-matched training experiments remain outstanding.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Building a structure from parts requires more than recognizing its final
appearance. A system may identify a brick yet misunderstand a connection,
produce a plausible exterior with a different hidden arrangement, or
specify a legal final assembly in an impossible construction order.
Conversely, failure to recover hidden geometry from an opaque image does
not necessarily imply a reasoning failure. A benchmark must make these
distinctions explicit before scores can support claims about model ability.

Prior work establishes strong foundations. BrickGPT generates buildable
structures from text using autoregressive prediction with validity
checking and physics-aware rollback~\cite{pun2025brickgpt}.
BrickNet expands the data and connection vocabulary through human-designed
LDraw objects and graph-backed assembly programs~\cite{kulits2026bricknet}.
Break and Make evaluates interactive understanding by inspecting,
disassembling, and rebuilding objects in LTRON~\cite{walsman2022break}.
BrickAtlas does not claim to originate generation, connection reasoning,
or disassembly-and-reconstruction. Its present focus is an explicit
evaluation contract connecting several task families to the same source
objects and making input privileges, alternative answers, and execution
evidence inspectable.

Three practical problems motivate this release. First, dataset size is
easily overstated by counting colors, views, and task variants as
independent objects. Second, ``ground truth'' may refer either to one
valid witness or to the only acceptable answer. Conflating them penalizes
legitimate alternatives. Third, a model's output can be improved or
reordered by the surrounding harness without that intervention being
visible in the reported result. A visualization of a final structure
being unfolded piece by piece is not evidence that the model planned its
assembly.

We address these problems with a procedural casebank, typed evaluation,
and auditable artifacts. The scope is deliberately digital: orthogonal
rectangular bricks and plates, nominal stud contact, and vertical
insertion/removal. We do not use a robot, manipulator, or
vision--language--action controller. We do not equate nominal support
with physical stability.

The current contribution package is:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A versioned resource with 5,120 geometrically distinct objects from
eight construction policies, preserving source-group membership across
all derived tasks and input conditions.
\item Per-case ground truth that records answer semantics, structural
targets, protected regions, nominal connection ports, executable
witnesses, and metric-specific oracle evaluations.
\item A complete deterministic baseline and replay audit, including
counterexamples that exposed ambiguous repair labels and metadata
leakage during development.
\end{enumerate}
These are implemented capabilities, not claims of state-of-the-art
performance or submission readiness. Neural results in this manuscript
come from earlier, much smaller releases and are labeled accordingly.

\section{Related Work and Positioning}
\paragraph{Generation and structural representations.}
BrickGPT~\cite{pun2025brickgpt}, previously released under the LegoGPT
name, couples a language model with external checks to generate stable
brick structures from text. BrickNet~\cite{kulits2026bricknet} collects
over 100,000 human-designed objects and scenes and models connectivity
through graph-backed programs. These works cover substantially richer
semantic or geometric settings than the current BrickAtlas grid domain.
Our relative-coordinate encoding, inherited from an earlier release,
is not a replacement for BrickNet's typed connector language. The present
casebank asks a different engineering question: how should multiple
tasks on shared objects be labeled, scored, and traced without silently
changing the information available to a model?

\paragraph{Interactive assembly.}
Break and Make~\cite{walsman2022break} already studies structural
understanding through disassembly and reconstruction. BrickAtlas's
earlier CARE-mini track also uses an interactive action loop, but the
v2 casebank evaluated here is primarily a fixed-input submission
protocol. A static structure answer, a submitted plan, a fixed
validation-feedback run, and autonomous tool use are separate tracks.
We do not merge their scores into a single agent leaderboard.

\paragraph{Training baselines.}
We use a public Qwen3-0.6B checkpoint~\cite{qwen3model} with standard
LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora} for an earlier text/structure pilot. This is
an implementation baseline, not a new model architecture. The experiment
does not train a vision encoder, does not use v2 data, and does not
establish compute-matched multi-task transfer.

\section{Casebank Construction}
\label{sec:data}
\subsection{Geometric Domain}
A part is represented as
\begin{equation}
p=(\mathrm{id},t,c,x,y,z,r),
\end{equation}
where $t$ is one of 25 catalog types, $c$ is one of six colors,
$(x,y,z)$ is the minimum body corner, and $r\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ is a
quarter-turn yaw. Horizontal units are studs (8\,mm), and vertical units
are plate heights (3.2\,mm). Coordinates are integers; the inherited
validator limits part origins to $[0,24]^3$, bodies to $[0,32]^3$,
and submissions to 64 parts. Generated v2 objects contain 9--47 parts.

Nominal validity requires no body overlap and at least one supporting
stud below every elevated part. Planning additionally checks unobstructed
vertical insertion or removal. All structures start with a common
4-by-8 plate; this improves controlled generation but restricts shape
diversity. Types and colors are public rather than novel categories.
Actual catalog coverage of assembled objects is smaller than the
25-type recognition catalog.

\subsection{Policies, Groups, and Splits}
We generate 640 accepted unique geometries per policy
(Fig.~\ref{fig:policies}). Tip walk attaches at the current tip with
one supporting parent; low fan samples low parents; shallow terrace
restricts attachment height more strongly; plate network uses thin
parts; column field constructs independent vertical profiles on a common
base; alternating beams switch beam orientation. Two-support bridge
explicitly constructs paired supports before spanning them, while
enclosed shell constructs a perimeter and roof around varying interiors.
These are different construction procedures, not a claim of eight
disjoint semantic distributions.

Geometry is canonicalized over color removal, translation, and four
global yaw rotations. Exact duplicate geometries are rejected both
within v2 and against the two earlier procedural releases. Mirroring
is not included in the equivalence group. All variants of an accepted
object inherit one group and split.

Six policies use a deterministic geometry-hash partition into training,
validation, and ID test objects. Bridge and shell policies are entirely
held out. This is a \emph{policy holdout}: it does not prove that primitive
motifs or geometric support are absent from training. Unlike an earlier
pilot whose differently named families sometimes shared sampling code,
v2 declares the actual policy distinction and its limits.

We also run approximate cross-split near-duplicate screening on canonical
part-position sets: 32 hash minima, eight bands, and a Jaccard threshold
of 0.8. The screening retrieves 48 candidate pairs and flags none above
threshold. This is not an exhaustive nearest-neighbor search; it can
miss near duplicates and does not measure semantic or subassembly leakage.

\subsection{Task Expansion and Accounting}
Each object produces 13 task variants and 23 task-condition cases:
two relation queries (connected and separated), one reconstruction,
one constrained generation, one suffix completion, three edits
(rotation, recoloring, removal), two plans (assembly, disassembly),
and three repairs (normal, color, shift).
Reconstruction, completion, and each repair variant have three
information conditions. Thus,
\begin{align}
N_{\mathrm{variant}}&=5{,}120\times13+150=66{,}710,\\
N_{\mathrm{case}}&=5{,}120\times23+150=117{,}910.
\end{align}
The extra 150 cases cover 25 known catalog types in six colors.
They form a separate recognition set rather than repeating the same
isolated image once per assembly. Color instances share the catalog-type
group when uncertainty is summarized.

\section{Ground Truth and Information Boundaries}
\subsection{A Witness Is Not Always a Unique Answer}
Every case stores a ground-truth object containing the public-input
hash, source group and split, target and starting geometry, bill of
materials (BOM), extents, connection edges, individual nominal stud
mates, changed and protected IDs, yaw symmetry, assembly and disassembly
witnesses, applicable metric names, and an evaluated oracle answer.
The record explicitly declares whether the answer is exact or whether
the oracle is only one member of an acceptable set.

For generation, the witness satisfies dimensions, zero anchoring,
piece budget, color count, connectivity, and branching constraints;
other satisfying layouts are accepted. For planning, any executable
permutation is valid. Structure reconstruction ignores arbitrary
instance IDs and symmetric yaw rotations. These equivalences are
different and are not collapsed into one similarity score.

\subsection{Three Information Conditions}
\paragraph{Ordinary RGB.}
Four assembled views (isometric, top, front, side) and a BOM are provided
for reconstruction, completion, and repair. Hidden layers are not
revealed. The primary structural criterion accepts matching colored
positive-axis first-hit surfaces plus BOM and nominal validity.
It therefore does not force one unobservable interior arrangement.
Preservation and localization gates remain mandatory where relevant.

\paragraph{Layer disclosure.}
An assembled view and separate top views for each occupied bottom-height
layer reveal additional information. Unlike ordinary RGB, this condition
requires the complete reference type/color/pose multiset, up to symmetry.
It is not pooled with ordinary visual performance.

\paragraph{Privileged symbolic reference.}
The complete reference program is explicitly supplied. This diagnostic
isolates failures that remain despite complete geometry information and
requires full structural correctness. It is not a visual benchmark score.
Other task families use their declared default inputs: symbolic structures
for relations, edits, and plans; constraints for generation; isolated
images for part recognition.

\subsection{Independent Checks and Label Repairs}
Generation uses the main axis-aligned geometry checker. A separately
implemented integer-cell checker independently traverses occupied cells,
support, collision, and insertion corridors. Connection stud totals must
agree. Every ground-truth witness passes this cross-check and its task
scorer. This independence is at the algorithm-implementation level; both
checkers share the declared catalog and nominal geometric assumptions.
It is not independent human validation.

Full-case baseline testing exposed a subtle repair ambiguity during
pre-release development. A shifted brick could occupy the same pose as
another brick of the same type and color. The reference image then
could not identify which of the two current IDs should be labeled faulty.
We exclude these indistinguishable shifted poses before generating the
release. Thirty cases exposed the issue in an initial symbolic repair
baseline. The final casebank and baseline were rebuilt and re-audited;
pre-release results are not reported as final scores.

Fault type, changed IDs, target programs, and private render geometry are
not carried in ordinary public input records. In particular, the
normal/color/shift variant label is evaluator metadata, not a prompt field.
The model exporter emits only a case ID, fixed system prompt, public
task, and image paths. The full case index and ground-truth shards remain
public research artifacts, so this separation is a protocol boundary,
not protection against a participant deliberately consulting the answers.

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:metrics}
\subsection{Structural and Task-Specific Metrics}
Let $T$ and $A$ denote expected and submitted part multisets, and let
$m$ be the number of exact type/color/pose matches after accounting
for yaw symmetry. Part precision, recall, and F1 are
\begin{equation}
P=\frac{m}{|A|},\qquad R=\frac{m}{|T|},\qquad
F_1=\frac{2m}{|A|+|T|}.
\end{equation}
The implementation defines empty-set conventions explicitly and prevents
duplicate predictions from inflating multiset matches. BOM metrics
ignore poses, while edge F1 requires matched endpoints and the correct
number of mating studs. Occupancy IoU is computed both without color
and on cell/color tuples.

For an ordinary visual case, let $S^+(T)$ be the set of colored
first-hit cells along the positive X, Y, and Z axes. The visible
structural predicate is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
V(A,T)={}&\mathbf{1}\{\mathrm{valid}(A)\}\\
 &{}\cdot\mathbf{1}\{\mathrm{BOM}(A)=\mathrm{BOM}(T)\}\\
 &{}\cdot\mathbf{1}\{S^+(A)=S^+(T)\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This is a nominal grid-surface proxy, not pixel similarity or a
ray-tracing proof of what is visible from every perspective.
Full-structure success is reported separately. A high surface F1 alone
does not imply success.

Completion and editing additionally measure preservation of the protected
source multiset. Completion reports precision/recall/F1 for additions
after subtracting retained source matches. These addition diagnostics
can disagree with a valid alternative hidden layout and therefore do
not override ordinary-condition acceptance. Repair additionally
requires exact faulty-current-ID localization, reports restoration and
localization metrics separately, and measures false alarms only on
normal cases.

Part recognition reports type, color, and stud-count accuracy. Relations
report direct connectivity, vertical ordering, contact-stud count, and
shortest undirected contact-graph distance. Constrained generation is
scored against its six public constraints and legality; similarity to
one arbitrary witness is not a generation-quality metric.

\subsection{Executable Plans}
A plan is evaluated in its submitted order. Execution stops at the
first unknown, duplicate, unsupported, colliding, or blocked action.
The scorer records the zero-based failure step, legal-prefix fraction,
executed coverage, unique submitted coverage, length accuracy, and
duplicate-free status. Complete success requires every required ID
exactly once and a fully executable sequence.

The evaluator does not sort a failed plan into a successful one.
An incomplete but locally legal prefix is distinct from an attempted
illegal action: its failure-step field is null, while success is zero.
This distinction is important for greedy algorithms that run out of
legal choices after prematurely covering a lower piece's insertion path.

\subsection{Denominators and Aggregation}
A selection manifest freezes case IDs, filters, and a hash before
scoring. Unknown or duplicate prediction IDs are rejected. Missing
submissions produce explicit failure rows and remain in all applicable
denominators. Malformed structure outputs receive zero structural
metrics; malformed QA can retain correct-field partial credit but
cannot receive exact success. A null metric means task-inapplicable,
such as a false-alarm rate on a positive repair case, not an omitted
failure.

Reports are stratified by task, variant, condition, and split, with
additional policy and piece-count summaries. Every metric carries its
actual denominator. Success uncertainty uses a fixed-seed, 1,000-resample
bootstrap of source-group means. No aggregate mixes ordinary images,
layer disclosure, and full symbolic references. These intervals quantify
sampling variation within this procedural resource, not semantic
coverage or immunity to contamination.

\section{Audit and Replay System}
The replay format separates four actors: model, harness, environment,
and evaluator. Events identify whether evidence was recorded or derived,
and link content-addressed before/after states. Recorded API responses
retain their original text and billing receipts. Decoding and
deterministic reconstruction are marked as system operations.
No unavailable internal reasoning or historical timestamp is invented.

For a structure collection, the viewer unfolds submitted parts as a
harness visualization, not as an assembly plan. For a plan, it executes
the original order and displays the first rejection. Input, intermediate
output, and reference views can be compared with synchronized cameras.
The input-only view hides the judge scenes and diagnostics. Per-case
JSONL export preserves events and manifests.

The v2 external-submission track stores submitted JSON, not a fabricated
provider transcript. Missing token counts, latency, and receipts are
explicitly unavailable; zero new calls during replay is not a claim
that an external model's inference was free. Deterministic baseline
outputs are labeled as harness/algorithm evidence rather than model
responses. Every one of the 117,910 stored baseline cases has been
replayed offline with score and state-chain checks; browser interaction
tests are representative rather than 117,910 manual UI inspections.

\section{Experiments and Current Results}
\subsection{Complete V2 Algorithmic Baseline}
The baseline accepts only public task input. It computes symbolic
relations, applies declared edit operations, greedily chooses an
immediately legal next plan action, and copies or compares an explicitly
supplied symbolic reference. It has no visual perception or constrained
generation solver. For unsupported inputs it abstains; the evaluation
records a zero rather than silently removing those cases. These
abstentions are coverage facts, not failed attempts by a visual model.

All 117,910 selected cases were processed. The baseline solves all
10,240 relation cases, all 15,360 edit cases, and all 25,600 privileged
symbolic reconstruction/completion/repair cases. It solves 2,364 of
5,120 assembly plans (46.2\%) and all 5,120 disassembly plans.
Table~\ref{tab:planning} separates the splits. Since this baseline is
not learned, the ID/policy-held-out contrast describes procedural
difficulty for a fixed algorithm, not learned OOD generalization.

The assembly/disassembly asymmetry is mechanistically interpretable.
Current insertion legality does not ensure that a later lower piece
will retain an unobstructed vertical corridor. A greedy strategy can
place a supported roof or bridge early and block that corridor.
Disassembly starts from the complete valid structure and removes an
eligible piece. The result shows that our process metric distinguishes
final geometry from executable construction; it does not show that the
tasks are hard for a suitable search or dependency-aware planner.

\subsection{Earlier Neural Pilots: Separate Evidence}
\label{sec:pilots}
Two preceding releases provide neural pipeline evidence but not a v2
leaderboard. A 576-object composition pilot evaluated four held-out
objects with GPT-4.1 mini and Gemini 2.5 Flash. Each model answered 56

diagnostic, not a statistically established model comparison.
V2 also changes some scoring rules, including full-structure requirements
under layer disclosure; cross-version scores must not be pooled.

\subsection{Verification and Resource Accounting}
All 117,910 v2 oracle answers pass and all empty answers fail. The
artifact verifier regenerates every ground truth and compares all
public inputs and private render recipes, checking 476,760 serialized
rows across 14 compressed shards. Ground truth is therefore materialized
and checked case by case, rather than inferred from a few examples.
A full offline replay independently re-evaluates the deterministic
baseline and reconstructs all event state chains.

Engineering validation passes 79 benchmark tests and 69 original
application unit tests, TypeScript checking, and production frontend
build. Browser checks cover 1,440-pixel desktop and 390-pixel mobile
layouts, nonblank canvas pixels, camera interaction, input/truth
separation, pagination, scoring, and replay export. A render audit tests
115 views across the largest sample in each policy. All dataset render
recipes are verified, but not all possible PNGs are pre-rendered.
A 46-case external-input export verifies 125 image uses and 25 unique
image hashes.

A copied environment without the original runtime directory or API key
regenerates the complete casebank audit and replays all evaluations.
It shares the installed pinned Node dependencies; this is not a clean
operating-system installation, a second-person reproduction, or a
repeated stochastic model training run.

The historical API campaign contains 363 settled requests totaling
\$0.45102511: \$0.10992152 for CARE-mini and diagnostics,
\$0.08908686 for Grid-v1 experiments, \$0.20204040 for the composition
pilot, and \$0.04997633 for the paired controls. V2 construction,
algorithmic evaluation, and verification add no API requests.
We preserve the cumulative ledger and its \$4.50 internal cap rather
than resetting the budget between releases.

\section{Limitations and Research Readiness}
\paragraph{Data and domain.}
The casebank contains procedural, not human-authored, structures and
shares a common root plate. More task conditions do not create more
independent objects. Eight policies do not imply broad semantic coverage.
The known-part recognition set is not a novel-part generalization test.
Policy-held-out bridge and shell data share primitives with training.
Near-duplicate screening is approximate.

\paragraph{Validity of ground truth.}
Two geometry implementations reduce some coding risks but share the same
nominal assumptions. No force, clutch, deformation, load-bearing
stability, or arbitrary CAD collision model is validated. Public BrickNet
connector annotations were imported in an earlier audit, but only the
25-type grid domain was cross-checked; the gated human-model dataset was
not obtained. Real independent human review is still absent. The
200-object review interface does not constitute 200 completed reviews.

\paragraph{Metric validity.}
The surface criterion reduces hidden-layout unfairness but is not a
complete model of image observability. It ignores some rendered seam
information and can differ from perspective visibility. Exact metrics
can be unforgiving to near-correct answers; partial metrics are included
to make that distinction visible. Additional calibration using
independent human judgments and adversarial alternatives is needed.

\paragraph{Empirical scope.}
No large neural evaluation has been run on v2. The complete baseline is
a deterministic public-input algorithm, with explicit abstentions for
unsupported modalities. Prior neural studies have only four shared
composition objects or eight older text cases. They cannot establish
cross-task correlations, representation advantages, or general
multi-task transfer. Broader model families, specialized baselines,
properly matched token budgets, and confirmatory sample designs remain
necessary before strong comparative claims.

\paragraph{Release and research ethics.}
Procedural model records declare CC0 provenance. Imported BrickNet
annotations retain MIT attribution, and the Qwen base checkpoint and
adapter documentation retain the Apache-2.0 context. Raw API receipts
and outputs are preserved without storing credentials or machine
identity. A future human study needs actual independent participants,
appropriate consent, and a documented disagreement process.
This manuscript uses the official CVPR 2026 template for exposition;
it is not a claim of submission, acceptance, or compliance with a
future conference year's rules.

\section{Conclusion}
BrickAtlas v2 turns a small multi-task prototype into a materialized,
versioned casebank with per-case ground truth, explicit information
conditions, complete metric denominators, and auditable replay.
Its full algorithmic baseline reveals a concrete gap between locally
legal greedy assembly and executable completion. The implementation
also exposes the limits of its present evidence: procedural diversity,
nominal geometry, and small earlier neural pilots are not substitutes
for independent data review and broad model evaluation. The current
release makes those next experiments reproducible and their assumptions
inspectable, without reporting them as already completed.
