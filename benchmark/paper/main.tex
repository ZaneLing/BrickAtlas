\begin{abstract}
A valid final brick structure does not certify a constructible process:
an intermediate state may be unsupported, a placement may leave no tool
clearance, a local error may require rollback, or missing evidence may
require clarification rather than a guess. BrickAtlas separates these
failures with explicit information and action contracts. Its historical
audit contains 5,120 procedural sources and 117,910 conditions; six
original 158--408-part objects support deeper development protocols.
The new Constructibility-1 release provides 78 sequence-audit, blocked-recovery,
stockout-replanning, and calibrated-clarification conditions. A body-only
insertion rule misses all six designed clearance dead ends. Public-information
solvers pass all conditions, while named shortcuts pass only 6/18, 0/18,
0/6, and 5/36 conditions respectively. Diagnostic-2 adds 138 paired
diagnosis--access--repair conditions, and a finite grammar supplies 600
ambiguity conditions with exhaustive answer sets. We retain raw outputs,
source groups, costs, and replayable evaluation evidence. Constructibility-1
uses nominal support and clearance proxies and has no new learned-model,
human, physical, or independent-source results; those remain required
before confirmatory claims.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A system can recognize the bricks in an object without recovering their
poses, match its exterior without identifying hidden connections, or
return a nearly unchanged structure with high similarity after failing
an edit. A correct final assembly also need not make a submitted
construction order executable. Treating these outcomes as a single
notion of ``structural intelligence'' obscures what a model accomplished.

BrickAtlas uses shared source assemblies to separate these endpoints.
Figure~\ref{fig:tasks} shows actual inputs and ground truth, while
Fig.~\ref{fig:execution} distinguishes a valid sequence from a dead end.
Following dataset/model reporting and holistic evaluation
principles~\cite{gebru2021datasheets,mitchell2019modelcards,liang2023helm},
we separate information, outputs, equivalences and efficiency.

This is not a claim to originate brick generation, reconstruction, or
multi-model assembly evaluation. BrickGPT~\cite{pun2025brickgpt} and
BrickNet~\cite{kulits2026bricknet} provide strong generative foundations;
MEPNet~\cite{wang2022manual}, Neural Assembler~\cite{yan2024assembler},
and TreeSBA~\cite{guo2024treesba} address visual assembly.
BC-Bench~\cite{liu2026composer} evaluates selection and pose;
PhyBlock~\cite{ma2025phyblock} covers diagnosis and planning robustness.
Our narrower opportunity
is \emph{measurement validity across shared objects}: when does a
plausible appearance, a high similarity score, or one valid reference
justify a downstream structural conclusion?

Our central question is whether a system can produce a process whose
prefixes remain geometrically legal, supported, accessible, and recoverable,
while requesting information when evidence is insufficient. We represent
this as the diagnostic vector $\mathcal{C}=(G,P,A,R,U)$ rather than a
weighted score that lets static geometry compensate for failed recovery.
Diagnostic-2 separately tests diagnosis--access--repair coherence.
Historical studies examine global versus changed-region similarity,
hidden-relation identifiability, and candidate-order sensitivity.

The release contributes (1) a process-aware constructibility contract
joining prefix validity, explicit tool clearance, minimum recovery,
finite-stock replanning and calibrated inspection; (2) a source-grouped
visual/structural evaluation with exhaustive finite-world answer sets;
and (3) full-population shortcut audits, paired controls, raw-output
replay and an eight-model descriptive development matrix. We separate
implemented capability, measured development evidence, and unrun
experiments throughout.

\section{Related Work and Position}
\paragraph{Generation and assembly.}
BrickGPT~\cite{pun2025brickgpt} generates stable and buildable objects
from text. BrECS~\cite{ahn2024brecs} studies budget-aware generation and
completion with efficient constraint checking. BrickNet~\cite{kulits2026bricknet}
uses human-designed LDraw objects and typed connectors; its 320,808
samples and 9,743 part variants far exceed our grid vocabulary.
Nominal contact in BrickAtlas is not a substitute for their structural
or physical scope.
MEPNet~\cite{wang2022manual} reads stepwise manuals;
Neural Assembler~\cite{yan2024assembler} predicts components, topology,
poses, and sequence from multiple views; TreeSBA~\cite{guo2024treesba}
uses a tree representation and synthetic-to-real supervision.
Break and Make~\cite{walsman2022break} studies interactive inspection,
disassembly, and rebuilding. Their input protocols differ from fixed
image or symbolic submissions and require audited adapters for comparison.

\paragraph{The closest multimodal benchmark.}
BC-Bench/Brick-Composer~\cite{liu2026composer} evaluates diverse-brick
selection and pose estimation from manual context and current state,
including feedback and training. LEGO-Puzzles~\cite{tang2025legopuzzles}
also includes multi-step plans; PhyBlock~\cite{ma2025phyblock} studies
progressive planning, diagnosis and counterfactuals. Co-builder~\cite{huang2025cobuilder}
addresses state detection, and WorkBenchMark~\cite{ma2026workbenchmark}
includes robot/VLA assembly. These are not empty research areas.
Our bounded distinction is a shared-object conjunction of per-prefix
support, explicit tool clearance, costed local recovery, stockout
continuation and observation-consistent uncertainty. It is not a claim
of first-ever assembly reasoning or robot execution.

\paragraph{Diagnostic visual reasoning.}
CLEVR~\cite{johnson2017clevr} establishes controlled compositional
reasoning evaluation; BLINK~\cite{fu2024blink} tests perceptual skills,
including multi-view and spatial reasoning.
CSGNet~\cite{sharma2018csgnet} predicts executable shape programs.
SpatialBabel~\cite{liu2026spatialbabel} examines reconstruction across
scene-code languages. Output-protocol effects are therefore not new
in themselves. Answer consistency also has prior work~\cite{hudson2019gqa,jing2022consistency};
our distinction concerns executable maintenance constraints.
Similarly, MCQ position bias is established~\cite{zheng2024selectors};
our candidate experiment specifically compares actual pose changes
against an identical-repeat baseline, without inferring internal
model mechanisms from external API replies.

\section{Benchmark Construction and Tasks}
\subsection{Geometry, Sources, and Splits}
A part is $(i,t,c,x,y,z,r)$: instance ID, type, color, minimum body
corner, and quarter-turn yaw. \textbf{$Y$ is vertical}; $X,Z$ use
8\,mm studs and $Y$ uses 3.2\,mm plate heights. Origins are integer
coordinates in $[0,24]^3$, bodies must fit in $[0,32]^3$, and submissions
contain at most 64 parts. Odd yaw swaps width and depth. Geometry is
nominally valid if bodies do not overlap and every elevated part has
at least one directly supporting stud. Force balance, clutch, arbitrary
CAD geometry, and robotic grasping are outside this contract.

Eight seeded policies accept 640 unique sources each, all starting
from a 4-by-8 plate. Canonicalization removes color and translation
and considers four yaw rotations, but not mirrors; within-release
and earlier-release exact duplicates are rejected.

Six policies use a deterministic geometry-hash split, while bridge
and shell are policy-held-out (Table~\ref{tab:splits}).
All task variants and prefixes retain the source group.
This is a procedural-policy holdout, not absence of shared primitives
or a semantic generalization guarantee. An exhaustive 8,305,836-pair
cross-split audit finds no canonical-token Jaccard similarity at least
0.8; it does not test best-aligned subassemblies or pretraining contamination.

Table~\ref{tab:tasks} defines the task families.
\textbf{Recognition} predicts a known catalog type, color, and stud
count from isolated views.
\textbf{Relations} uses a symbolic assembly and two IDs to ask direct
contact, vertical ordering, mating-stud count, and shortest undirected
contact-graph distance; a non-contact pair can still have a graph path.
\textbf{Reconstruction} predicts a full structure from reference
information and BOM.
\textbf{Generation} accepts any legal structure satisfying exact
extents, zero anchoring, a piece budget, color count, connected
component count, and minimum maximum contact degree. It is constrained
layout generation, not semantic text-to-shape evaluation.

\textbf{Completion} removes the last $\lfloor n/4\rfloor$ pieces of
a valid insertion sequence and requires preserving the remaining
prefix. It does not cover arbitrary holes.
\textbf{Editing} rotates the whole structure, replaces all occurrences
of one color, or removes one topmost piece. Rotation maps positive
$X$ toward positive $Z$, keeps $Y$, and reanchors minimum $X,Z$.
\textbf{Repair} has no-fault, color-fault, and one-stud-shift cases
on exposed topmost pieces. It requires corrected geometry and faulty
current IDs, with an empty fault list for a normal input.
\textbf{Planning} supplies the target with IDs in lexical order and
requests any executable permutation for vertical assembly/disassembly.

Each source yields 13 variants and 23 information-conditioned cases.
Reconstruction, completion, and all three repairs have three reference
conditions. With 25 catalog types in six colors, the total is
$5{,}120\times23+150=117{,}910$.
\textbf{Ordinary RGB} provides isometric/top/front/side assembled views
and BOM; interiors remain hidden. \textbf{Layer disclosure} provides
an assembled view and top images of each bottom-$Y$ layer with other
layers removed. \textbf{Symbolic reference} supplies complete geometry.
The latter two add information and require full type/color/pose recovery.
Other families use their declared symbolic or constraint inputs.
Fault labels, changed IDs, and private render recipes are excluded
from ordinary prompts.

\section{Ground Truth and Measurement}
\subsection{Visual Compatibility Versus Exact Structure}
Ground-truth records bind input hashes, source groups, target/source
geometry, protected and changed IDs, contacts, nominal stud mates,
executable witnesses, and metric applicability.
Multiset matching ignores arbitrary output IDs and symmetric per-part
yaw, but does not silently align global translation or rotation.
For $m$ matched parts in target $T$ and answer $A$,
\begin{equation}
P=m/|A|,\quad R=m/|T|,\quad F_1=2m/(|A|+|T|).
\end{equation}
BOM F1 removes pose; occupancy IoU removes part boundaries; edge F1
requires matched endpoints and mating-stud counts.

For ordinary reconstruction, let $S^+$ be colored first-hit cells
along positive $X,Y,Z$. We accept
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
V(A,T)={}&\mathbf{1}\{\mathrm{valid}(A)\}
\mathbf{1}\{\mathrm{BOM}(A)=\mathrm{BOM}(T)\}\\
&{}\cdot\mathbf{1}\{S^+(A)=S^+(T)\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This proxy allows alternative hidden arrangements; it is not arbitrary
camera visibility or pixel similarity. Full-reference correctness is
reported separately. Completion subtracts preserved source matches
before computing \emph{addition} F1. Edits/repairs require protected
piece preservation; repairs additionally require faulty-ID localization.
False alarms are defined only for no-fault cases.

\paragraph{RQ1: no-operation shortcuts.}
Copying a prefix with $k$ missing parts from an $n$-part target gives
\begin{equation}
F_1^{\rm copy}=\frac{2(n-k)}{2n-k},\qquad F_{1,\rm added}^{\rm copy}=0.
\end{equation}
Copying before a one-piece removal similarly yields
$2(n-1)/(2n-1)$, approaching one without performing the edit.
A full 35,840-case no-operation census confirms this: 87.1\% completion
and 97.4\% removal F1, both with zero task success.
Positive repair copies reach 94.9\% F1 without correct localization.
Normal no-fault repair correctly succeeds unchanged.
All seven endpoints contain one case per source; per-case results
and complete global/local metrics remain available.

\subsection{An Ambiguity-Aware Extension}
\label{sec:ambiguity}
Matching appearance need not identify hidden relations. Instead of
forcing one reference answer, we make the admissible set explicit.
The new, separately versioned track uses sealed shells with a
$2\times W$ internal layer, $W\in\{2,4,6\}$, tiled by identical
1-by-2 bricks. Three stack heights give nine parameterized families.
The tilings are exhaustive within this finite grammar: their counts
are 2, 5, and 13, independently checked by the recurrence
$c_W=c_{W-1}+c_{W-2}$, $c_0=c_1=1$.
There are 60 valid layout states across the nine families.

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/brick-ambiguity.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Same exterior, different ground truth.}
The two sealed structures have identical rendered exterior pixels
under the tested cameras. Exploding their layers reveals horizontal
versus vertical red internal bricks; side walls are omitted in the
disclosed views. The outlined adjacent blue pair
shares one red supporter in A but not B. The exterior-only answer is
therefore \texttt{undetermined}; disclosing the support layer makes
the answer definite. Exploded views are evaluator illustrations.}
\label{fig:ambiguity}
\end{figure*}

For each pair of blue upper bricks we ask whether they share a
directly supporting red brick. Given the observation $O$ and public
grammar $\mathcal{G}$, let $\mathcal{A}(O,\mathcal{G})$ be all admissible
layouts. With Boolean query $q$,
\begin{equation}
y(O)=
\begin{cases}
\mathrm{yes},&\forall A\in\mathcal{A},\ q(A)=1,\\
\mathrm{no},&\forall A\in\mathcal{A},\ q(A)=0,\\
\mathrm{undetermined},&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Exterior and support-disclosed conditions create 600 paired cases
(Table~\ref{tab:ambiguity}). Ninety exterior questions are genuinely
ambiguous within the declared grammar; disclosure resolves them.
Known geometry and the grammar are supplied symbolically, so this
extension tests observation-consistent structural inference, not
pure visual perception. Public records omit labels and hidden
supports; an enumeration solver consumes only these public inputs
and solves all 600 cases.

\subsection{Execution and Evaluation Integrity}
\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=.95\textwidth]{figures/brick-execution.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Ground-truth construction versus a designed dead end.}
Four snapshots follow the curated double-span bridge's verified
height-sorted sequence. The last two panels contrast a legal prefix

the missing inner pier can no longer descend vertically. The evaluator
retains the submitted order and first rejection rather than repairing it.}
\label{fig:execution}

Execution stops at the first unknown, duplicate, unsupported, colliding,
or blocked action. We record legal-prefix fraction, coverage, duplicate
status, and the zero-based failure step. An incomplete legal prefix
fails success but has no illegal step.
Height sorting solves all 10,240 plans in this vertical domain:
supporters are lower and already present; an earlier part overlapping
the footprint must end below the new part, or the final geometry would
collide. Reversing the sequence gives disassembly.
This establishes a constraint-following ceiling, not difficult search.

Frozen selection manifests reject unknown/duplicate predictions;
missing/malformed submissions remain in applicable denominators.
Null means inapplicable, not a removed failure. A versioned strict
decoder closes legacy primitive-type coercion without altering recorded
scores. All 117,910 oracle answers pass; independent metrics and
mutation checks are detailed in the supplement. Replay labels model,
harness, environment, and evaluator operations separately. Visual
unfolding of an unordered structure is never called a model plan.

\subsection{Candidate Consistency and a Visual Control}
Coordinate-normalized two-piece probes compare full reconstruction,
local pose, candidate selection, and privileged symbolic answers. A
12-source, four-model follow-up separates reordering from repetition.
For selected poses
$a_g,a_g^r,a_g^\pi$, the paired endpoint is
\begin{equation}
D_g=\mathbf{1}[a_g^\pi\ne a_g]-\mathbf{1}[a_g^r\ne a_g].
\end{equation}
Mean excess mismatch is 0.50--0.75 across models, with all 192
requests retained. Source-bootstrap intervals~\cite{efron1979bootstrap}
are in the supplement. Mismatch is inconsistency, not incorrectness.

A known-renderer mask-matching control uses public BOM and candidates,
not target geometry. It yields 44/44 image-conditioned successes and
16/16 no-image abstentions. This is a signal control, not a general reconstructor.

\section{Experimental Coverage and Remaining Work}
Diagnostic-2's public symbolic control passes 36 maintenance cases;
a known-renderer color control passes their 36 layer-image counterparts.
It reads public pixels/current geometry, not target JSON, and is not
a general pose estimator. Constructibility-1 public solvers pass all
78 conditions. Always-valid, no-operation, unavailable-part reuse and
always-\texttt{h0} controls pass 6/18, 0/18, 0/6 and 5/36 respectively;
the first six are the valid sequence controls. These checks establish
executable, nonconstant task signals, not model discrimination.
Independent objects, human review and matched-budget model experiments
remain necessary.

\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
BrickAtlas is currently a controlled grid benchmark, not a comprehensive
semantic or physically validated assembly benchmark. V2 retains its
common base, 19 assembled types, suffix-only completion, topmost repairs,
and height-solvable planning. Frontier raises size to 408 parts, but retains
the 25-type rectangular vocabulary and has no learned-model measurements.
Constructibility-1 adds per-prefix support and a one-stud side-clearance
proxy, not force balance, grasping, or continuous robot motion. Its local
rollback and stockout tiling remain exactly solvable; size alone does not
prove difficult reasoning or empirical discrimination.
The ambiguity extension
is exhaustive only within its stated grammar; nine parameter families
are not independent external sources. Pixel equality is verified on
one renderer, not every camera or human observer.
Current human-review packets contain no real submitted judgments.
Small adaptive model studies and same-machine replay do not replace
independent confirmation and second-environment reproduction.

Within this scope, the benchmark makes useful distinctions executable:
changed-region success is not global similarity~\cite{geirhos2020shortcut}; an admissible structure
does not determine every hidden relation; and final validity does not
certify prefix support, tool clearance, recovery, or calibrated commitment.
Stronger claims require independent licensed sources, matched visual
process inputs, learned-model and human evidence, and physical validation.
