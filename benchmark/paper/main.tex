\begin{abstract}
Brick reasoning involves both object complexity and task composition.
Increasing the number of views or edits does not create independent
objects, and naming a task ``physical'' does not validate its mechanics.
We introduce Hierarchy-3, a corrected public development release of BrickAtlas
crossing 48 original layouts with 48 shared task families.
Four structural strata contain twelve layouts each; every layout supports
22 atomic, 15 metacognitive/physical, six procedural, and five
integrative tasks, for 2,304 questions on 6,339 visible parts.
A state-transition interpreter checks submitted actions, while
resource and possible-world validators evaluate scheduling and
conditional policies. Rapier supplies actual collision queries and
sampled dynamic traces for all objects. The original BrickAtlas website
now exposes every layout as interactive 3D geometry, with exploded
inspection and playback of submitted actions.
A low-cost pilot yields \PilotSuccess{} successes on \PilotN{} fixed questions for
GPT-4.1 mini at \$\PilotCost. This tests pipeline functionality,
not calibrated difficulty or a model ranking. The revision corrects
ambiguous choices, geometry envelopes, inter-module penetrations and replay state.
All 48 layouts satisfy the new nominal-state protocol, whose finite-force
servos and conservative geometry remain explicit limitations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Digital brick environments offer discrete object identities, hierarchical
assemblies, and explicit edits. These properties support reproducible
evaluation across perception, structural reasoning, and intervention.
However, benchmark growth can obscure rather than resolve a model's
failure: more derivatives of a few objects create larger denominators
without broader source coverage. Likewise, a final correct object does
not show that an agent constructed, repaired, or inspected it correctly.

BrickAtlas organizes evaluation along two axes. The vertical axis
describes the structural scope of an object: component, assembly,
mechanism, or system. The horizontal axis describes task composition:
atomic operations, metacognitive and physical reasoning, executable
procedures, and integrated constraints. Crucially, the same 48 families
occur at every level, so comparisons can hold task identity fixed.
Figure~\ref{fig:matrix} shows the complete crossed design.

This release corrects the 40-layout Hierarchy-2 library and adds eight
compositions, preserving its six Mechanism-1 objects and crossing every
source with 48 tasks. These are 48 layouts, not 48 independently sampled mechanical families: new layouts
reuse authored components. OMR/LDraw examples inform visual design but
are not benchmark cases. The retired 5,120 procedural sources remain
historical regression data and are excluded from the new scale.

Three implementation changes improve inspectability. First, all
physics-derived questions use archived Rapier observations rather than
handwritten response tables. Second, action answers execute a public
state machine; an equivalent legal order can pass without matching a
single reference list. Third, dataset geometry is available inside the
original application, allowing inspection of modules, individual
parts, exploded configurations, and intermediate action states.

We make the following contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a balanced four-by-four organization with 48 authored layouts
and 48 task families repeated on every layout;
\item executable action, schedule, and possible-world contracts with
negative controls and explicit physical-evidence boundaries;
\item an integrated 3D browser sharing model data with the offline
release and displaying reference or submitted programs; and
\item a versioned correction audit, semantic-answer baselines, and
per-object geometry and nominal-state validation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
BrickGPT~\cite{pun2025brickgpt} addresses stable brick generation;
BrickNet~\cite{kulits2026bricknet} uses typed connection graphs.
BrECS~\cite{ahn2024brecs} studies sequential assembly with budgets.
Hierarchy-3 evaluates an existing design and its interventions; it does
not claim comparable asset scale or a new generative method.

LEGO-Puzzles~\cite{tang2025legopuzzles} and
PhyBlock~\cite{ma2025phyblock} cover visual, spatial, ordering, and
counterfactual reasoning. Manual-based assembly methods such as
MEPNet~\cite{wang2022manual}, Neural Assembler~\cite{yan2024assembler},
and TreeSBA~\cite{guo2024treesba} recover poses or construction steps.
Our contribution is a shared task-by-source organization with executable
contracts, rather than a claim that each individual family is new.

Break and Make~\cite{walsman2022break} evaluates interactive disassembly
and reconstruction. WorkBenchMark~\cite{ma2026workbenchmark} and
BC-Bench~\cite{liu2026composer} address embodied or compositional
assembly. We explicitly separate digital state validity from real
insertion force, connector tolerance, grasping, and hardware transfer.

\subsection{Representation and Provenance}
A part has a shape, size, local position, quaternion, color, and module
membership. Modules have world poses, masses, roles, and anchoring
status. Joints identify endpoints, local anchors, type, axis, and limits.
The visible vocabulary includes bricks, plates, beams, windows, slopes,
arches, cylinders, wheels, axles, gears, and spheres.

These shapes are LEGO-like digital primitives, not verified commercial
part specifications. Original-site model JSON and offline model JSON
are byte-identical. Each object also records its family and lineage;
future train/test splits must group related layouts and reused components.
The present public development set has no hidden confirmation split.

\subsection{Matched Coverage}
Every level has 576 questions. Atomic tasks are repeated across levels,
including recognition, counting, local edits, topology, and pose
corrections. Meta tasks include both physical-record interpretation
and uncertainty-aware decisions, with new nonuniform posteriors,
expected-loss decisions and force-moment calculations. Procedural
families include assembly, disassembly, supported service repair,
compound editing, guarded repair and rollback.
Integration adds exclusive-tool repair and budget-constrained policies
to distributed repair, scheduling and inspection-conditioned policies.

Task identifiers bind source and family. Changing a render, color,
answer order, or fault creates a condition within a source, not a new
source object. Although all families are crossed, many local questions
remain easy with explicit structured input; a larger scene alone does
not guarantee a harder local operation.

\section{Task and Scoring Contracts}
\subsection{Atomic and Metacognitive Questions}
Atomic tasks return a choice ID. Each distractor changes a relevant
field, set, count, identity, or transformation. Option rotation is
deterministic per task. Set-valued answers are canonicalized before
deduplication; ordered vectors and programs retain their ordering.
Some questions have fewer than four distinct alternatives; chance
baselines must use the actual option count.

Multi-answer tasks require the entire feasible set. In accessibility,
an empty feasible set is valid if every proposed path collides.
Counterfactual support excludes the explicitly removed object and
recomputes reachability to any anchored root through remaining joint
edges, allowing redundant support paths.

Inspection uses a finite uniform prior and deterministic observations.
For query $q$ with observation partition sizes $n_o$ over $N$ worlds,
\begin{equation}
U(q)=\frac{-\sum_o (n_o/N)\log_2(n_o/N)}{c(q)}.
\end{equation}
All utility-maximizing queries are accepted. Abstention tests whether
all compatible worlds imply the same action. Posterior tasks condition
the finite prior on an observed query result. Pareto tasks return every
non-dominated alternative under cost, mass, and stiffness.
Additional tasks use explicit nonuniform prior weights and asymmetric
failure losses. Six previously constant-answer families now vary
their actual parameters and semantic answers; the release reports
per-family semantic-majority baselines, not just letter-choice baselines.

Public inputs explicitly disclose structural metadata where required.
Therefore most atomic scores measure structured reasoning with
auxiliary images, not isolated visual perception. Image-removal,
symbolic-removal, and viewpoint ablations are necessary before claiming
visual reasoning gains.

\subsection{Executable Procedures}
An action is a tuple
\begin{equation}
a=(P_a,F_a,A_a,D_a,c_a),
\end{equation}
with required facts, forbidden facts, add effects, delete effects, and
cost. From state $S$, execution requires $P_a\subseteq S$ and
$F_a\cap S=\varnothing$, then applies
\begin{equation}
S'=(S\setminus D_a)\cup A_a.
\end{equation}
The interpreter checks every action in submitted order, stops at the
first invalid transition, and records its cause. Success requires
all goal facts and the declared budget.

Assembly places a module only after its declared predecessors.
Disassembly removes children before parents. Repair sequences support,
open, remove, replace, verify, close, and release an interface.
Compound editing substitutes a color operation within a protected
service sequence. Independent actions can commute. Duplicate placements,
unknown IDs, missing terminal steps, or budget overruns fail.
Guarded repair and rollback expose unsafe shortcuts that cannot satisfy
verified goals. Exclusive-tool tasks remove a shared availability fact
until the active intervention releases the tool.

The transition contract certifies symbolic intervention rules.
It does not automatically simulate robot motion or tool contact for
every repair action. The website changes module visibility and colors
according to these same transitions and displays state facts alongside
the 3D replay, so presentation and scoring share one interpreter.

\subsection{Integrated Constraints}
Distributed repair combines multiple independent faulty modules under
one budget and one final goal. Scheduling requires integer start times,
predecessor completion, non-overlapping use of exclusive workcells,
and completion by a deadline. Inspection policies select a query and
map every possible observation to an action; the validator checks all
worlds instead of only a sampled rollout.

No LLM judge determines correctness. Empty or malformed outputs remain
failures. A batch evaluator must retain missing cases in its frozen
denominator and reject unknown or duplicate case IDs. Per-family and
per-source reporting is preferred to a pooled percentage.

\section{Physics and Quality Audit}
Rapier 0.20.0 runs at 120\,Hz with 64 solver iterations. Visible parts
are conservatively approximated by stud-inclusive cuboids and grouped into rigid
bodies. Fixed, revolute, prismatic, and spring descriptors instantiate
the declared mechanism. Bases are anchored; holding motors use public
force-based stiffness 60,000, damping 1,500 and maximum force 20,000
in scene units. A separate static world performs three continuous service
shape casts per object, excluding the serviced module.

After 120 nominal steps, the audit records a geometric representative
point's displacement, angular displacement, and non-spring joint residuals.
All 48 objects satisfy fixed thresholds of 0.35 scene units, 0.15 radians,
and 0.02 scene units respectively. Every rendered vertex lies within
its envelope. All joint anchor pairs initially coincide, and oriented
cuboid contact queries find no penetration above 0.005 between any
module pair, including directly joined modules. Joint contacts remain
enabled. Revolute limits are set and read back on instantiated joints,
with a driven over-limit regression control.

Corrections relocate interfering service modules, rudders, drills,
racks, pumps and transfer stages, repair four inconsistent tower
anchors, and add separated elastic moorings to the flood-response barge.
The simulator protocol also changes from the earlier 120/20/100 holding
motor settings. Therefore improved outcomes cannot be attributed solely
to geometry corrections. Active finite-force servos are not passive
stability or real LEGO material calibration.

Impulse application and response measurement use the geometric
representative point, remaining invariant under a change of local origin.
An off-center impulse is followed by 120 simulation steps, sampled at
eleven time points. Dynamic questions ask for the maximum of the
\emph{sampled translational response}, not the continuous maximum, a
rotational safety bound, or a calibrated load threshold. They cannot
be used to claim real-world robustness.

Other checks cover unique identities, finite poses, positive dimensions,
declared dependency order, rendered-image hashes, and exact website/
dataset parity. Inter-module bounding-box overlaps are published as
diagnostics rather than silently treated as precise mesh collisions.

Deterministic checks accept all 2,304 reference answers. A separate
public-precondition solver succeeds on 384 action tasks. The simple
constant-choice/empty-answer control succeeds on \ControlSuccess/2,304 cases,
with heterogeneous choice cardinalities and output contracts.
Reference acceptance is evaluator validation, not learned-model
performance; the action solver has privileged symbolic rules compared
with a purely visual agent.

The original application's homepage lists all 48 benchmark models.
Each opens an orbit-controlled 3D workspace with zoom, pan, canonical
views, module isolation, explosion, and image export. Its task panel
shows all 48 contracts, and action JSON can be replayed with a step
slider and first-failure feedback. Selection and isolation preserve
the current frame's active modules. The same renderer generates 192
audited model views. Every source and every task family is indexed in
the supplement; full question/choice/input/answer records are shipped
in the Chinese question bank and machine-readable release.

\section{Limitations and Next Validation}
The release substantially increases coverage and inspectability, but
the structural levels remain authored categories. There is no human
timing study, item-response calibration, or causal proof that higher
levels are harder. Shared constructors create correlated layouts.
The 16-question pilot is too small for population accuracy, robust
ranking, or level-wise discrimination claims.

Physical realism is limited by compound bodies, cuboid envelopes,
anchored bases, and uncalibrated material parameters. Digital primitive
connectivity is not commercial LEGO clutch-force validity. Passing
the declared nominal-state protocol does not certify arbitrary
motion, passive balance, or extreme loads. Symbolic repair transitions
and exploded display layouts are not collision-free robot trajectories.

Future confirmation requires source-family-disjoint hidden designs,
manual geometry review, calibrated connectors and contact mechanics,
human difficulty measurements, and information-condition ablations.
These are outstanding scientific requirements, not outcomes inferred
from passing software tests.

\section{Conclusion}
Hierarchy-3 provides a common source-by-task organization for brick
reasoning, execution, and integrated decisions. Its 48 layouts,
2,304 questions, state-based scoring, actual simulator records, and
original-site 3D access make the benchmark easier to inspect and
extend. Versioned errata preserve historical inputs and scores while
the corrected release remains explicit about physical and statistical boundaries.
