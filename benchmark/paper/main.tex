\begin{abstract}
Reasoning about brick assemblies requires more than recognizing an
object's silhouette: an evaluator must identify the exact part, preserve
its geometry, and distinguish what is visible from what is structurally
known. We introduce BrickAtlas LDraw-1, a benchmark of \LDrawSources{}
original LDraw/OMR assemblies, \LDrawParts{} part instances, and
\LDrawTasks{} questions across eleven task families. A stable instance
number links rendered observations, source records, connector graphs,
and executable scene edits. The task engine separates visual perception,
source-data reasoning, graph reasoning, and instruction-following edits,
with explicit evidence and success contracts. We provide real 3D inputs,
functional task decompositions, source-level statistics, and a
reproducible evaluation protocol. Independent reparsing verifies every
retained source instance; all reference solutions and 75 public-action
solver outputs satisfy their contracts. Answer-prior analysis exposes
uneven task coverage and elementary evidence controls. These results
validate the software interface, not learned-model ability or physical
stability. Model, human, robustness, and physical-validation experiments
are specified with unfilled result tables. The release makes assembly
reasoning inspectable while retaining unresolved structural evidence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
An assembly assistant must connect language to individual physical
parts. ``Remove the support'' is ambiguous when several pieces have the
same shape and color. A convincing rendering can hide disconnected
geometry. A connector graph can support an exact adjacency answer while
remaining insufficient to predict whether an object will stand under
gravity. Evaluating all three cases with a single notion of assembly
success obscures the source of an error.

Recent work has advanced interactive reconstruction~\cite{walsman2022break},
manual interpretation~\cite{wang2022manual}, and graph-backed brick
generation~\cite{kulits2026bricknet}. These settings motivate a
complementary question: can a model ground a request in a fixed,
authentic assembly and reason correctly under the evidence provided?
This question is useful even before introducing robotics. An assistant
that selects the wrong instance or treats missing connector data as proof
of a floating part cannot provide reliable downstream instructions.

We introduce \textbf{BrickAtlas LDraw-1}. Its starting point is an
unchanged original source assembly, not a generated arrangement that
resembles one. Each occurrence of a part receives a stable
\texttt{B0001}-style identifier. Questions, numbered renders, public
evidence, action execution, and review records share that identity.
Repeated part types remain separate instances. The source montage in
Fig.~\ref{fig:sources} shows vehicles, creatures and larger structures;
all are rendered from retained LDraw geometry.

Our design separates four observation contracts. Visual tasks identify
color or corresponding part types from numbered images. Source-data
tasks use supplied coordinates, coverage flags or author records.
Graph tasks operate on recognized connector pairs. Scene-edit tasks
restore an instance's visibility or follow a source-step window at fixed
poses. This separation makes exact evaluation possible without treating
an editor operation as a mechanically feasible insertion.

The release has three contributions. First, it provides a
source-preserving corpus with instance-level grounding and attributable
dependencies. Second, it defines eleven inspectable task families,
including set-valued graph answers and executable action sequences.
Third, it provides a reproducible evaluation specification, descriptive
analyses and failure-review artifacts that distinguish measured evidence
from experiments still to be conducted. The current manuscript is a
benchmark development draft: it reports no learned-model leaderboard
and no claim of certified physical stability.

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Brick representation and assembly.}
Break and Make~\cite{walsman2022break} evaluates interactive structural
understanding through disassembly and reconstruction. Translating a
Visual LEGO Manual~\cite{wang2022manual} connects visual instructions to
executable plans. BrickNet~\cite{kulits2026bricknet} represents typed
interfaces and generates assemblies with connector graphs; we use its
public connector implementation to audit fixed source placements.
BrickGPT~\cite{pun2025brickgpt} targets text-conditioned stable, buildable
structures. LDraw-1 evaluates questions over existing sources and does
not reproduce those methods' generative or robotic results.

\paragraph{Compositional visual reasoning.}
CLEVR~\cite{johnson2017clevr} and GQA~\cite{hudson2019gqa} make question
semantics and compositional operations explicit. We adopt this principle
for numbered CAD instances, but do not inherit their balancing
procedures or validation results. BLINK~\cite{fu2024blink} emphasizes
basic perceptual failures, while LEGO-Puzzles~\cite{tang2025legopuzzles}
studies multi-step spatial reasoning. A source part number in our
reference solution is a CAD identity, not a guarantee that all visually
equivalent parts share one label.

\paragraph{Physical reasoning and evaluation.}
PhyBlock~\cite{ma2025phyblock} evaluates physical understanding and
planning under its simulation contract. Our graph and scene operations
have narrower semantics: recognizing a hinge does not establish its
load capacity or simulated dynamics. Dataset documentation
principles~\cite{gebru2021datasheets} motivate explicit provenance,
coverage and intended use. Shortcut learning~\cite{geirhos2020shortcut}
and option-order sensitivity~\cite{zheng2024selectors} motivate the
answer-prior and permutation controls specified below.

\section{Benchmark Representation}
\subsection{Source Identity and Numbered Observations}
An assembly is a collection
\begin{equation}
M=\{(b_i,p_i,c_i,T_i,h_i)\}_{i=1}^{n},
\end{equation}
where $b_i$ identifies an instance, $p_i$ is its LDraw part type, $c_i$
its source color, $T_i$ its source transform, and $h_i$ its hierarchy
path. Nested MPD submodels are expanded with source file and line
provenance. Dependency closure and geometry are preserved. A documented
axis and unit conversion maps LDraw coordinates to the viewer's
millimeter frame~\cite{ldraw,threejs}.

Let $H$ be the original file's SHA256 digest. The number
$\ell_i=\texttt{B}\Vert\mathrm{pad}_4(i)$ maps to one instance within
$H$; $(H,b_i)$ is the complete identity. Numbering does not identify a
type or describe a role. Thus two identical pins have different
references even when a language description would be identical.
Fresh source parsing verifies the stored type, color, transform and
hierarchy for every instance.

Numbered leader lines connect the relevant IDs to projected geometry.
Fig.~\ref{fig:numbers} shows an operand-isolation view from set 42004.
At most twelve labels appear simultaneously; each operand remains
individually accessible through inventory lookup and isolation.
Occlusion is not solved by a label alone. The interactive contract
therefore permits rotation, zoom and isolation, while the static
contract uses a frozen numbered input. All 140 visual questions have
an exported operand image. Their public records omit source attributes
and answers.

\subsection{Typed Structural Evidence}
The connector audit produces a multiset $R$ of records
$(b_i,b_j,k_i,k_j,f,\theta)$, where $k_i,k_j$ index connectors, $f$
is an interface family and $\theta$ contains matching parameters.
BrickNet 0.1.0 recognizes stud, fixed, axle, hinge and ball families.
For graph questions we use the simple undirected projection
\begin{equation}
E=\{\{b_i,b_j\}:\exists r\in R\text{ linking }b_i,b_j\}.
\end{equation}
Repeated connector records do not increase the degree. Unsupported
definitions remain explicit. Definition coverage measures catalog
support, while connected components describe the recognized graph;
neither is a complete physical connectivity score.

Connector inference uses the upstream alias rules and tolerances.
Its parser normalizes rotation matrices, so we record the maximum
deviation for each source. Rendering retains the original transforms,
and inset-mesh queries use the original alias-transformed poses.
Intersections are candidate evidence: friction-fit and gear geometry
can intersect an approximation without constituting a defect.
The release retains \LDrawIntersections{} pairs without recognized
mating interfaces for further adjudication. No source parts are moved
to improve these counts.

\subsection{Executable Task Contract}
A task $q=(H,\mathcal{B},o,f,x,y^\star)$ binds source $H$, numbered
operands $\mathcal{B}$, observation contract $o$, family $f$, public
input $x$ and reference output $y^\star$. Model-facing records contain
the first five fields, with answers stored separately for review.
Visual, source-data, graph and scene-edit inputs are denoted V, S, G
and E, respectively.

For scene edits, a state contains facts $F$ and a visible instance set
$V$. An action $a$ specifies required facts $R_a$, forbidden facts
$B_a$, additions $A_a$, deletions $D_a$ and cost $c_a$. It is legal if
$R_a\subseteq F$, $B_a\cap F=\varnothing$, and its cumulative cost
fits the task budget. Its fact transition is
\begin{equation}
F'=(F\setminus D_a)\cup A_a.
\end{equation}
The corresponding display transition changes visibility at original
poses. Unknown actions, repeated completed steps, missing prerequisites
and budget overruns are rejected. A rejected transition leaves the
display state unchanged. Goal and prohibited facts define success.

\section{Task Construction}
\subsection{Functional Families and Applicability}
Table~\ref{tab:operators} decomposes every family into its operations.
The four organizational layers are atomic, metacognitive, procedural
and integrative. They contain 213, 201, 130 and 73 tasks, respectively.
These labels describe the intended operation, not an experimentally
calibrated hierarchy of difficulty. In particular, some metacognitive
items are elementary checks of an explicit evidence statement.

The generator samples instances deterministically from each source,
with a size-dependent cap of two to five targets. It creates tasks only
when the evidence supports the contract. Type correspondence requires
another instance of the same source type and different-type distractors.
Distance questions use geometry bounding-box centers, not mass centers,
and reject near ties. Connector classification supplies the record's
family explicitly; its purpose is evidence reading, not visual contact
inference.

Neighbor questions require the exact set of adjacent candidates.
Graph-deletion questions remove a vertex and incident edges from the
supplied local induced graph, then count its connected components.
Disconnected distractors can contribute to that count. This operation
does not imply that the selected brick can physically be extracted
without moving others. Coverage questions identify unsupported
definitions, and evidence-limit questions ask whether the supplied
audit establishes gravitational stability. The latter always has the
semantic answer ``not established by this evidence.''

There are 469 single-choice, 73 multiple-choice and 75 action tasks.
Options are deterministically shuffled by task identity. This removes
a fixed construction order but does not balance semantic answers.
The applicability rules produce unequal counts across families and
sources; we retain those differences rather than manufacture tasks
without their prerequisites.

\subsection{Six-Step Worked Example}
Seventeen sources contain usable author STEP provenance. Expanded
author steps preserve source hierarchy and ordering; editorial groups
or file-list positions never substitute for instructions. The
source-sequence family takes the first usable window of up to six
steps and encodes predecessor facts. It evaluates following the
supplied author order, not discovering a new assembly plan.

Fig.~\ref{fig:steps} shows all six states for set 42102 in reading order.
Table~\ref{tab:steps} lists the newly revealed IDs and state facts. Step
lookup asks where an instance first appears; step sequence asks for the
legal ordered program. Color, type and distance families bind numbered
parts in the source, while graph deletion operates on supplied
connector evidence. These are different questions about a common
assembly, not six unmeasured levels of physical difficulty.

\section{Corpus and Descriptive Analysis}
\subsection{Acquisition, Scale and Diversity}
The preparation inventory contains 43 candidates, of which 29 complete
sources were converted. Fourteen were excluded for recorded preparation
issues such as unresolved geometry, attribution gaps, size limits,
singular transforms or loader identity mismatch. Five further sources
were excluded for duplicate placements or definition coverage below
90\%. This threshold limits unsupported graph evidence; it is not a
physical acceptance probability. The remaining \LDrawSources{} sources
represent \LDrawSources{} set families. Unknown parts remain in their
original scenes.

Table~\ref{tab:scale} gives the scale bands. The corpus contains 1,002
distinct source part-type strings and 49 color codes; these are
representation vocabularies, not canonical mechanical equivalence
classes. Source sizes range from 53 to 1,978 instances, with median
274. Fig.~\ref{fig:distribution} plots the empirical distributions and
the relation between instance count and type count. Larger scenes do
not simply repeat one small part inventory.

We retain one primary build per set family. Geometry fingerprints
remove labels, colors, instance ordering and global translation;
all retained fingerprints differ. The 276 source pairs also receive
an inventory screen,
\begin{equation}
J(A,B)=\frac{\sum_p\min(c_A(p),c_B(p))}
{\sum_p\max(c_A(p),c_B(p))},
\end{equation}
where $c_A(p)$ counts occurrences of type $p$. The largest observed
Jaccard value is 0.450, between the large tractor and jet. A
centered, scale-normalized nearest-center score supplies an additional
shape screen. Both are descriptive: shared inventories and orientation
sensitivity prevent interpreting them as proof of independence.

\subsection{Coverage and Structural Heterogeneity}
Fig.~\ref{fig:coverage} reports all family-by-band counts. D3 has only
two sources, whereas D4 has eight; source-order families additionally
depend on author metadata. Consequently, a pooled score can change
with source mix even if within-family performance is unchanged.
Per-family and source-macro reporting are necessary.

Fig.~\ref{fig:connectors} gives within-source connector composition.
The audit records 34,877 matches: 27,132 stud, 6,976 axle, 411 hinge,
326 fixed and 32 ball records. Stud interfaces dominate globally, while
Technic sources contribute more axle and joint evidence. Records can
repeat a part pair; these counts are not independent contacts and
do not measure mechanical strength.

\subsection{Answer Priors and Elementary Controls}
Fig.~\ref{fig:priors} compares single-choice priors by family. Across
469 items, always choosing A yields 26.652\% accuracy; uniform choice
has expected accuracy 27.342\%, accounting for each item's option
count. These values should not be generalized to exact-set or action
outputs. The evidence-limit family has a constant semantic answer,
so high accuracy there alone provides little evidence of reasoning.
Graph-deletion answers are also concentrated. Per-family performance
must therefore accompany any aggregate.

Semantic concentration is computed in-sample for shared answer
vocabularies; instance-valued answers have no cross-source semantic
majority. It is a diagnostic, not a held-out baseline. No balancing or
adversarial filtering result is claimed. Future balancing must be
versioned so that the present questions and measured counts remain
reproducible.

\section{Evaluation Protocol}
\subsection{Conditions, Metrics and Reproducibility}
The proposed primary evaluation uses each task's designated public
input: V (140 items), S (169), G (233), and E (75). Static and
interactive visual results must be reported separately. An image-only
system should not be scored as if it received the graph evidence, and
a text-only control should not receive answer-bearing visual source
attributes. Every run must freeze its checkpoint or API revision,
prompt, decoding settings, image resolution, tool budget, timeout and
task manifest.

Single-choice success is exact option identity; multiple-choice success
requires the exact set, with duplicate IDs invalid. Action success
requires a legal complete execution and the specified goal state.
Formatting errors, refusals and timeouts remain failures in the
denominator and receive separate reason codes. Let $s_q\in\{0,1\}$ be
task success and $Q_m$ the eligible questions for source $m$:
\begin{align}
\mathrm{Micro}&=\frac{\sum_q s_q}{|Q|},\\
\mathrm{Macro}&=\frac{1}{|\mathcal M_Q|}
 \sum_{m\in\mathcal M_Q}\frac{\sum_{q\in Q_m}s_q}{|Q_m|}.
\end{align}
For a restricted modality or family, $\mathcal M_Q$ contains only
sources with eligible questions. Report exact success, valid-output
rate and action-goal success separately. A partially correct neighbor
set may receive secondary set F1, but does not count as exact success.

Tasks within a source are correlated. Proposed confidence intervals
resample source groups rather than individual questions, using a fixed
seed and 10,000 bootstrap replicates~\cite{efron1979bootstrap}.
For trained methods, folds must group all instances, views, questions
and alternative builds by source set; neighboring source inventories
require additional leakage inspection. No train/test split or
confidence interval is presented as already established.

\subsection{Measured Validation}
Independent parsing reproduces all \LDrawParts{} source instances.
Checks cover identities, types, colors, transforms, hierarchy,
dependencies and mesh hashes. All \LDrawTasks{} reference answers pass
their contracts, all 75 action tasks are solvable from public
preconditions, and 1,606 invalid controls are rejected
(Table~\ref{tab:controls}). These checks establish consistency between
construction and execution. They do not establish perceptual grounding,
task difficulty or physical truth.

Every source loads its complete instance count in the viewer. Captures
include four canonical views, numbered operand images and worked
action frames. Interaction checks exercise isolation, explosion, source
replay, rejected operations and review persistence. Review records
bind the source hash, task ID, numbered operands, decision, reason and
timestamp. A failed review requires a reason; software validation never
writes a human approval.

Robustness tests permute IDs consistently across operands, overlays,
evidence and scoring, or permute option order while preserving semantic
answers. Camera changes test visual sensitivity. Color changes apply
only to type-correspondence questions that explicitly ignore color,
and remain evaluation copies of fixed sources. Equivalent edge-order
changes test serialization sensitivity. Paired consistency measures
agreement after mapping outputs back to canonical identities; correct
agreement and consistently wrong answers must be distinguished.

Human review should separately assess ID legibility, visual answer
ambiguity, source attribution, graph semantics and intersection
candidates. Independent dual review, agreement and adjudication counts
are proposed, with no human-pass rate yet available. Physical validation
requires additional assumptions about materials, contact and loads;
its results must remain separate from question accuracy.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
LDraw-1 exposes a useful distinction between object authenticity,
evidence completeness and answer correctness. Preserving a real source
prevents arbitrary geometric edits from silently changing the question,
but does not prove that contributor-authored CAD is physically valid.
The 573 unresolved intersection candidates, unsupported definitions and
inference tolerances remain material limitations. Source scenes can
also contain several legitimate independent objects.

The corpus is small and uneven. Several families are evidence-reading
controls, and the public-action solver's perfect contract success shows
that these action tasks do not demand discovering mechanical plans.
Original part-type strings can distinguish visually near-identical
variants; ambiguous correspondence items require human adjudication.
D1--D4 encode size, not measured cognitive difficulty.

Commercial sets may be familiar from pretraining. Public review
artifacts include answers, so the development website is not a hidden
test server. Evaluation must restrict models to designated inputs and
audit tool access. Earlier synthetic-release model pilots and physical
claims are excluded from this release.

\section{Conclusion}
BrickAtlas LDraw-1 provides a common identity and evidence interface for
visual, structural and procedural questions over original assemblies.
Its current contribution is a reproducible corpus and executable
evaluation specification, supported by measured source statistics and
software controls. The unfilled model and validation tables identify
the empirical work needed before claiming model rankings, calibrated
difficulty or physical reliability.
