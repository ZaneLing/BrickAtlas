\begin{abstract}
Recognizing a brick assembly, reconstructing its hidden parts, modifying
it, and specifying an executable build order are different problems.
Their scores become misleading when input privileges, acceptable
alternative structures, or evaluator interventions are left implicit.
We present BrickAtlas, a controlled benchmark resource that connects
eight task families through 5,120 procedural source assemblies and
117,910 task-condition cases. Explicit contracts separate ordinary
RGB, layer disclosure, and privileged symbolic references, and distinguish
visual compatibility from full structural recovery and plan execution.
We introduce a source-level coverage audit and evaluate 35,840
copy-current controls: doing nothing obtains 87.1\% part F1 on completion
and 97.4\% on removal, while solving neither task. A height-sorted
planner solves all 10,240 planning cases, delimiting this domain as
constraint following rather than difficult search.
An exploratory two-model study on 24 assemblies exposes reconstruction
floors and editing ceilings. Four-model candidate diagnostics on 12
additional development sources show more pose inconsistency under
reordering than identical repetition. We release reproducible contracts,
case-level outputs, figures, and audits, and explicitly separate these
findings from the still-unmeasured independent confirmation, external-domain,
and human-calibration experiments.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
What does it mean for a vision--language model to understand an
assembly? A correct part list does not locate the parts. A matching
exterior need not determine the interior. A high similarity score after
an edit may simply preserve what was already correct. Even an accurate
final configuration does not certify that a submitted construction
sequence can execute. These distinctions matter when visual reasoning
systems are evaluated as if recognition, structured prediction, and
action planning were interchangeable.

Brick assemblies make this problem concrete. They have discrete
identities, poses, contacts, and operations, enabling explicit evaluation.
At the same time, occlusion and repeated interchangeable components
make one reference program an incomplete definition of correctness.
A benchmark must specify both \emph{what information was available}
and \emph{which answers that information justifies}.
Figure~\ref{fig:overview} illustrates our evaluation contract.

Existing work provides strong generation and assembly capabilities:
BrickGPT produces stable, buildable structures from
text~\cite{pun2025brickgpt}; BrickNet learns graph-backed programs from
human-designed LDraw assemblies~\cite{kulits2026bricknet}; MEPNet parses
visual manuals into component poses~\cite{wang2022manual}; and Break and
Make studies interactive structural understanding~\cite{walsman2022break}.
BrickAtlas does not replace their richer geometry, learned methods, or
interactive settings. Its focus is a \emph{measurement problem}:
which conclusions survive when observations, answer equivalences, and
execution are accounted for explicitly on shared objects?

We organize the study around three questions.
\textbf{RQ1:} When does a high global structure score fail to indicate
that the requested operation was completed?
\textbf{RQ2:} Does visual compatibility determine downstream structural
relations?
\textbf{RQ3:} Are candidate-selection outcomes stable to a change in
presentation, beyond instability under an identical repeated request?
Each has an explicit negative outcome: similarity may agree with task
success, admissible layouts may agree on a query, or permutation may
be no less stable than repetition. No preferred model ranking is assumed.

Our contributions are threefold.
First, we provide an eight-family casebank with source-group splits,
typed answers, information conditions, and distinct visual, structural,
and executable success criteria.
Second, we provide full-population shortcut and coverage audits,
a constructive hidden-structure counterexample, and original-order
execution checks. These expose limits of the benchmark as well as
limits of simple solvers.
Third, we provide replayable exploratory model evidence and a concrete,
unfilled confirmation matrix. This is a benchmark-development study,
not a new model architecture or a claim of broad model superiority.

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Brick generation and reconstruction.}
BrickGPT~\cite{pun2025brickgpt}, initially named LegoGPT, combines
autoregressive generation with validity checks and physics-aware rollback.
BrickNet~\cite{kulits2026bricknet} expands beyond voxel-like towers to
human-designed objects and typed connections. Its paper reports 320,808
samples and 9,743 part variants; those units are not directly equivalent
to our deduplicated procedural source objects or 25-type catalog.
Neither their dataset scale nor their physical scope can be claimed
by importing only a small connector annotation subset.
MEPNet~\cite{wang2022manual} uses component geometry and stepwise
manual images for high-precision pose prediction. Its observation
protocol differs from reconstructing an entire opaque assembly.
Table~\ref{tab:position} summarizes the relevant distinctions without
interpreting an unreported capability as absent.

\paragraph{Visual programs and diagnostic reasoning.}
CSGNet~\cite{sharma2018csgnet} parses shapes into executable constructive
solid geometry programs, establishing that visual-to-program prediction
is not new. CLEVR~\cite{johnson2017clevr} uses controlled scenes and
functional questions to diagnose compositional reasoning. BrickAtlas
extends this diagnostic perspective to shared assembly objects, changed
regions, admissible hidden layouts, and execution order. This controlled
setting trades semantic realism for inspectable variables; procedural
scale alone cannot justify generalization to natural images or arbitrary CAD.
BLINK~\cite{fu2024blink} evaluates perceptual tasks including spatial
relations and multi-view reasoning with human and specialist baselines.
Our complementary target is executable, editable assembly structure;
we do not claim that general visual perception is untested.

\paragraph{Presentation bias and training controls.}
Option-position sensitivity is established in language-model
evaluation~\cite{zheng2024selectors}. Our candidate study therefore
does not claim to discover that bias. It compares \emph{actual selected
poses}, not answer letters, against a same-order repetition baseline
in a geometric domain. We do not infer token-level mechanisms from API
outputs. Earlier Qwen3~\cite{qwen3model} and
SmolVLM~\cite{marafioti2025smolvlm} LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora} pilots are
implementation controls, not proposed architectures or evidence of
multi-task transfer.

\section{The BrickAtlas Benchmark}
\label{sec:data}
\subsection{Domain and Source Construction}
A part is $p=(i,t,c,x,y,z,r)$: instance ID, catalog type, color, minimum
body corner, and quarter-turn yaw $r\in\{0,1,2,3\}$. The vertical axis
is \textbf{$Y$}; $X,Z$ are horizontal. Horizontal units are 8\,mm studs;
vertical units are 3.2\,mm plate heights. Odd yaw swaps the catalog
width and depth. Origins are integer-valued in $[0,24]^3$, bodies must
fit in $[0,32]^3$, and a submission contains at most 64 parts.
Rectangular bricks have height three and plates height one.
Nominal validity requires non-overlap and at least one supporting
stud for each elevated part. It does not establish force balance,
clutch strength, deformation, or robotic grasp feasibility.

We accept 640 unique geometries from each of eight seeded construction
policies (rendered gallery in the supplement). Tip walk extends a single supporting
parent; low fan favors low attachments; shallow terrace limits height;
plate network uses thin parts; column field varies independent columns;
alternating beams alternates orientation. Two-support bridge constructs
paired supports before a span, and enclosed shell places a perimeter
and roof around varying interiors. All sources start with a 4-by-8
plate. The generator normalizes minimum $X,Z$ to zero and checks
insertion validity and an independently implemented cell certificate.

Geometry is canonicalized after removing colors, translating, and
enumerating four global yaw rotations. Exact duplicates within the
release and against two earlier releases are rejected. Mirroring is
not part of this equivalence. Every task, color condition, or derived
prefix retains its source group; a new question is not a new independent
object.

questions. Thus $5{,}120\times23+150=117{,}910$ cases; counting variants
without repeated information conditions gives 66,710, not another dataset.

\paragraph{Recognition and relations.}
Recognition uses isolated isometric/top images and returns type, color,
and top-stud count. It is known-catalog identification, not novel-part
generalization. Relations supply the full symbolic assembly and two
IDs. One sampled pair has direct contact; the other lacks direct contact.
The latter can still be connected by a longer graph path.
Outputs include direct connectivity, vertical ordering, mating-stud
count, and shortest undirected contact-graph distance.
``Above'' means the entire first body starts at or above the second
body's top, not overlap in a projected image.

\paragraph{Reconstruction and constrained generation.}
Reconstruction returns a complete assembly matching its reference
condition and bill of materials (BOM). Generation instead receives exact
extents, zero anchoring, a piece budget, minimum color count, connected
component count, and a minimum maximum graph degree. Any nominally valid
structure satisfying these constraints is correct; similarity to a
particular generated witness is not a generation-quality score.
The current task is constraint generation, not natural-language
semantic shape generation.

\paragraph{Completion, editing, and repair.}
Completion removes the last $\lfloor n/4\rfloor$ parts of the valid
generation order and asks for the full restored structure while
preserving the prefix. It tests suffix completion, not arbitrary holes.
Edits comprise a whole-object quarter-turn, replacement of every
instance of a selected color, and removal of one topmost piece.
Rotation maps positive $X$ toward positive $Z$, retains $Y$, and
reanchors minimum $X,Z$ to zero.
Repair has no-fault, wrong-color, and one-stud-shift variants;
positive faults affect an exposed topmost part, not arbitrary hidden
corruption. It requires both a corrected structure and the faulty
\emph{current} IDs. Variant labels and changed IDs are evaluator
metadata, not ordinary prompt fields.

\paragraph{Assembly and disassembly.}
The target program is supplied with IDs sorted lexically, preventing
the source insertion order from directly serving as the answer.
A model returns all IDs once in its chosen order. Only vertical
insertion/removal is allowed. These tasks test order under known
geometry, not visual reconstruction, motion planning, or grasping.

\subsection{Information Conditions}
\label{sec:information}
\textbf{Ordinary RGB} provides opaque assembled isometric, top, front,
and side views plus BOM. The three axis views look from positive
$Y$, $Z$, and $X$, respectively. Cameras use orthographic projections
and recenter/rescale the assembly; screen pixels are not absolute grid
coordinates. Ordinary success accepts legal BOM-matched alternatives
with equal colored positive-axis first-hit grid surfaces.
\textbf{Layer disclosure} provides an assembled view and one top image
per occupied bottom-$Y$ layer with other layers removed; it requires
full type/color/pose recovery.
\textbf{Symbolic reference} supplies the complete target program and
also requires full recovery. It is privileged diagnostic information,
not a visual score. All three retain applicable preservation and
localization requirements. Render recipes and ground-truth records
are never appended to ordinary model inputs.

\section{Evaluation and Measurement Validity}
\subsection{Accepted Answers, Not Only Reference Programs}
Let $T$ and $A$ be target and answer multisets, and $m$ the number of
type/color/pose matches after part-yaw symmetry. Instance IDs do not
affect these structural matches. We report
\begin{equation}
P=m/|A|,\qquad R=m/|T|,\qquad F_1=2m/(|A|+|T|).
\end{equation}
Multiset matching prevents duplicate predictions from increasing recall.
BOM F1 removes poses; occupancy IoU removes part boundaries; colored
occupancy preserves colors. Edge F1 requires matched endpoints and
the correct number of mating studs. These quantities answer different
questions and are not averaged into one ``intelligence'' score.

Let $S^+(A)$ be the colored first-hit cell set along positive $X,Y,Z$.
For ordinary visual cases, the base structural predicate is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
V(A,T)={}&\mathbf{1}\{\mathrm{valid}(A)\}\\
 &{}\cdot\mathbf{1}\{\mathrm{BOM}(A)=\mathrm{BOM}(T)\}\\
 &{}\cdot\mathbf{1}\{S^+(A)=S^+(T)\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
It is a grid-surface proxy, not pixel identity or a proof of observability
from arbitrary cameras. Full-reference success is reported separately.
For completion, retained source matches are subtracted before computing
addition precision/recall/F1. Editing and repair require protected-piece
preservation; repair also requires exact faulty-ID localization.
False alarms are defined only on no-fault cases.

\paragraph{RQ1: why global F1 can reward doing nothing.}
For a completion with $n$ target parts and $k$ missing parts, copying
the current $n-k$ pieces yields
\begin{equation}
F_1^{\mathrm{copy}}=\frac{2(n-k)}{2n-k},
\qquad F_{1,\mathrm{added}}^{\mathrm{copy}}=0.
\label{eq:copy}
\end{equation}
With $k\approx n/4$, global F1 is already about $6/7$. For removing one
piece, copying all $n$ original parts instead yields $2(n-1)/(2n-1)$,
approaching one as assemblies grow. This is a property of the metric,
not evidence that the model performed the operation.

\paragraph{RQ2: matching observations need not match relations.}
For the declared admissible set $\mathcal{A}(T)=\{A:V(A,T)=1\}$, a
downstream query $q$ has a determined answer only if $q(A)$ is constant
over $\mathcal{A}(T)$. Figure~\ref{fig:hidden} gives a constructive
counterexample. Two hidden tilings have identical BOM, colored occupancy,
and declared first-hit surfaces, but different contact graphs and a
different answer to whether two upper pieces share one lower support.
Therefore an ordinary accepted reconstruction cannot automatically
inherit every relation label from the reference. This is one existence
witness, not a prevalence estimate, human judgment, or pixel-equality
claim. V2 relation questions avoid this ambiguity by supplying symbolic
geometry; extending them to images would require additional observations
or set-valued/undetermined answers.

\subsection{Executable Order and a Simple Complete Baseline}
Plans execute in the submitted order and stop at the first unknown,
duplicate, unsupported, colliding, or blocked action. We record
the zero-based failure step, legal-prefix fraction, executed coverage,
unique submitted coverage, length accuracy, and duplicate-free status.
An incomplete but legal prefix fails exact success and has no illegal
step. The evaluator never sorts a failed plan into a passing one.

\paragraph{Height-order sufficiency.}
Within the valid rectangular grid domain, sorting parts by increasing
bottom $Y$ gives a complete assembly order. Every supporter lies strictly
lower and is already inserted. Any earlier part overlapping the new
footprint must end at or below its bottom, otherwise the final structure
would overlap; hence no earlier part blocks insertion from above.
Equal-height non-overlapping parts can be ordered arbitrarily.
Reversing this sequence gives a valid disassembly order.
The complete 10,240-case execution audit agrees with this construction.
Consequently, failures of a myopic greedy planner demonstrate the
importance of order but do not establish hard combinatorial search.

\subsection{Denominators, Reproducibility, and Independent Checks}
A selection manifest freezes IDs, filters, and hashes before scoring.
Unknown/duplicate prediction IDs are rejected. Missing or malformed
submissions stay in applicable denominators; null denotes inapplicability,
not an omitted failure. A versioned strict primitive-type decoder
closes a legacy JavaScript coercion gap without rewriting historical
results. All 117,910 oracle answers still pass.

Every ground-truth record links public-input hash, source/split, target,
protected/changed IDs, contacts, nominal stud mates, metric definitions,
and an executable witness. Separate implementations check cell geometry,
structural metrics, and relation answers, with mutation/invariance tests
for malformed submissions. These reduce coding risk but share catalog
and nominal assumptions; they do not replace human calibration.
Detailed counts and replay contracts are in the supplement.

Replay distinguishes model output, harness operations, and evaluator
diagnostics; unfolding a structure for display does not count as a
model plan. Uncertainty uses 1,000 fixed-seed bootstrap resamples of
source-group means, keeping derivatives clustered and information
conditions separate.

\section{Experiments and Findings}
\label{sec:experiments}
\subsection{Full-Casebank Algorithmic and Shortcut Audits}
The public-input deterministic baseline solves every relation and
edit case and all 25,600 privileged symbolic reconstruction, completion,
and repair cases. It abstains on unsupported visual and generation
inputs; those zeros indicate coverage, not failed visual reasoning.
Its immediately-legal greedy planner solves 2,364/5,120 assemblies
(46.2\%) and all disassemblies. A roof can be placed legally before a
lower piece and block its future insertion corridor. Height sorting
solves both directions completely. Split-specific results are retained
in the supplement; because neither planner is learned, their split
contrast is not learned generalization.

We newly evaluate a copy-current predictor on \textbf{35,840 cases},
one ordinary completion, three symbolic edits, and three ordinary
repairs per source. Its predictor sees only public \texttt{current};
it returns that structure unchanged and an empty fault list for repair.
Table~\ref{tab:copy} is a census of the full released population,
including training objects, not a new held-out model experiment.
The 87.1\% completion and 97.4\% removal F1 scores confirm
Eq.~\ref{eq:copy}. Zero addition F1 or target success correctly reveals
that the requested change was not performed.
No-fault repair is the intended exception: leaving a correct structure
unchanged is the correct response.

\subsection{Coordinate and Output-Protocol Diagnostics}
A three-source screen adds GPT-4.1 and Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct.
A nested-prefix follow-up reveals inconsistent origin wording; the
supplement documents a separately versioned correction without
replacing original scores.

Four unused sources then provide distinct normalized two-piece scenes,
gray bases, blue query parts, known identities, and explicit camera/grid
conventions. Four models answer six conditions each (96 calls).
Full-structure and local-pose success both total 8/16; shorter output
alone is not supported as a general remedy. Candidates have equal
identities, yaw, and height and differ in legal horizontal position.
Only 6/16 paired candidate answers preserve the actual pose after
reordering. These four-source probes lack an identical-repeat control,
motivating the next experiment.

\subsection{RQ3: Candidate Reordering Beyond Repetition}
A follow-up freezes 12 new validation sources, two per seen policy,
with distinct normalized two-piece geometries. Original, identical
repeat, permutation, and no-image conditions yield 192 calls across
four models. Correct-option positions and repeat/permutation timing
are counterbalanced. For source $g$, let $a_g,a_g^r,a_g^\pi$ denote
selected \emph{poses}, after mapping option labels back to content.
The paired endpoint is
\begin{equation}
D_g=\mathbf{1}[a_g^\pi\ne a_g]-\mathbf{1}[a_g^r\ne a_g].
\end{equation}
Mismatch measures inconsistency, not incorrectness; a consistently
wrong answer has zero mismatch. Figure~\ref{fig:order} reports the
paired means and source-bootstrap intervals.

Repeat/permutation mismatches are 2/9 for GPT-4.1 mini, 2/11 for

Excess mismatch ranges from 0.50 to 0.75.
The result supports presentation sensitivity beyond the observed repeat
baseline in this development sample. Candidate construction, provider,
time, simple geometry, and prior adaptive protocol design remain
limitations; the shared 12 sources do not become 48 independent objects
by evaluating four models.

A post-hoc renderer-aware analysis-by-synthesis control enumerates
legal two-piece scenes from public BOM and candidate poses and matches
blue masks in three orthographic views. It solves all 16 original
sources across the four-source and 12-source studies and preserves
all 16 selected poses after permutation. All 44 image-conditioned
answers succeed; those are repeated conditions, not 44 sources.
No-image inputs abstain. Replacing views changes 9/15 jointly answered
selections, with one abstention. This establishes usable simulator
signal in this narrow domain, not general reconstruction or human
observability. The method uses the exact renderer and extra compute;
its enumeration, thresholds, and resource use are disclosed.

\section{Unmeasured Confirmation and Extensions}
The preceding evidence is useful for developing a benchmark but does
not supply independent confirmation. The supplement provides seven
deliberately empty experiment tables: an eight-task main-result layout
for new shared sources, paired information/representation controls, candidate
invariance and debiasing, difficulty/nuisance factors, external-domain
and specialized methods, human calibration, and effective training.
The machine-readable plan marks every unmeasured value as null;
neither the 24-object pilot nor scores from other papers can substitute
for these missing experiments.

Confirmation requires licensed assets, calibrated metrics, effective
baselines, and frozen sources, models, inputs, and analysis.
Specialized methods need matching task information. Sample size follows
precision, not desired significance; the supplement specifies
precision calculations and missing-pair bounds.

\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
\paragraph{Scope and remaining validation.}
All current source assemblies are procedural, share a root plate, and
use only 19 assembled types. Recognition covers known parts only.
Support is nominal, not physical stability; vertical planning admits
a simple complete order. The surface criterion omits some rendered
boundary information and does not determine hidden relations.
New image-conditioned relation tasks require an ambiguity-aware contract,
not automatic inheritance of hidden reference labels.
No licensed human-designed structure layer has been integrated.

The review interface contains 96 candidates from eight source objects,
but no completed human labels or adjudications. Independent reviewers
must measure false acceptance/rejection, disagreements, and unresolved
cases against a frozen scoring version. Same-machine, runtime-free
replay is not independent second-environment reproduction. The current
model studies are small, adaptive, and confound some geometric factors.
Training losses and engineering test counts do not establish construct
validity, positive transfer, or broad ranking reliability.

\paragraph{Release and research practice.}
Procedural records declare CC0 provenance; other assets retain their
own licenses. Credentials and human-review identities are not published.
The new census and figures make no API calls; historical resource
accounting is in the supplement. This development paper uses the official
template, not fabricated submission metadata or an acceptance claim.

BrickAtlas separates visual compatibility, structural recovery, local
change, and executable order on shared objects. Its audits expose
misleading global similarity, while model diagnostics motivate
controlled follow-up. Independent confirmation, human calibration,
and external-domain evidence remain necessary for stronger claims.
