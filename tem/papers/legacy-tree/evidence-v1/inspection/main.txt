An Instance-Aligned Benchmark on LDraw Assemblies

Abstract
A correct answer about a part does not establish that
001
a vision-language model followed the evidence identify-
002
ing that part.
The answer may follow a prior, persist
003
after relevant evidence changes, or change under an ir-
004
relevant display perturbation.
We introduce BrickAtlas,
005
an instance-aligned measurement protocol on 24 human-
006
authored LDraw assemblies with 15,334 placed parts. Its
007
main question is whether answers about the same target
008
correctly follow changed visual evidence and remain cor-
009
rect under speciﬁed semantic invariances.
The 617-item
010
resource separates 140 visual questions, 146 graph ques-
011
tions and two classes of controls. Paired observations bind
012
canonical identities, exact inputs, condition-speciﬁc gold
013
answers and answerability decisions. New-evidence accu-
014
racy and paired both-correct distinguish adaptation from
015
disruption. An audit of the unchanged graph questions il-
016
lustrates the need: counting nodes alone attains 86.30%
017
accuracy, but zero both-correct on answer-changing graph
018
pairs, while a graph algorithm solves both sides. A separate
019
matched-graph extension controls additional surface statis-
020
tics. We report veriﬁed corpus and algorithmic evidence;
021
visual model comparisons and human answerability review
022
remain unrun. The contribution is a reproducible resource
023
and falsiﬁable protocol, not a completed model ranking.
024
1. Introduction
025
In a dense assembly, two pieces can share geometry while
026
differing in color, position or identity. A question about one
027
piece therefore asks more than whether a familiar shape is
028
present: an answer must remain bound to the designated in-
029
stance. If the visible assignment of labels to pieces changes,
030
a model should follow the new evidence. If only the names
031
used to identify pieces change consistently, the semantic an-
032
swer should remain correct. Ordinary accuracy observes
033
neither distinction.
034
This motivates our primary research question: for the
035
same identiﬁed part in an original assembly, do answers
036
correctly follow task-relevant visual changes and remain
037
correct under speciﬁed answer-preserving changes?
We
038
study Color and Part-type separately. Two supporting ques-
039
tions concern the total effect of hiding non-operands at a
040
ﬁxed camera and whether graph answers follow relations
041
beyond elementary input statistics.
042
Human-authored LDraw models [4] provide useful test
043
conditions: repeated instances, densely nested assemblies,
044
author step records and geometry at preserved source poses.
045
Instance identity links these assets without requiring them
046
all in one question. Figure 1 illustrates the distinction. A vi-
047
sual question uses a numbered image; a separate graph ques-
048
tion uses an explicit edge list; a visibility edit uses declared
049
actions. Shared identity permits controlled inspection, but
050
does not imply a within-item multimodal reasoning chain.
051
Related resources already supply important ingredients.
052
BrickNet [3] has instance and connector graphs; LEGO-
053
Puzzles [6] uses editable scenes and step-consistent renders;
054
PhyBlock [5] combines scene identity, dependencies and
055
simulated assembly. Our distinction is the explicitly paired
056
measurement: an identiﬁed target, exact observations, two
057
semantic golds, and a decision on whether each observation
058
makes the question answerable. A change in old-gold ac-
059
curacy alone cannot establish that an answer followed new
060
evidence.
061
The present empirical evidence is algorithmic. On all
062
73 natural deletion questions, a predictor that ignores edges
063
and returns the node count minus one answers 63 cor-
064
rectly. On the corresponding ﬁxed-node, answer-changing
065
pairs it answers neither pair jointly correctly; connected-
066
component algorithms solve both. This post-hoc replication
067
exposes a concrete interpretive gap between high accuracy
068
and evidence following. It is not a result about a learned
069
model.
070
Our contributions are threefold. First, we make same-
071
instance visual evidence following measurable on un-
072
changed source assemblies, with modular structural and ex-
073
ecution controls. Second, we deﬁne paired semantic mea-
074
sures and source-balanced estimates that separate correct

083

088
and display actions. Table 1 compares the three closest as-
089

093

Distinct placed instances and connectors are al-
094

095
