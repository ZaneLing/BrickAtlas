\begin{abstract}
Recognizing a part and solving a structural problem about it are
different abilities. An end-to-end answer can obscure where their
composition fails. BrickAtlas evaluates this interface through
visually bound minimum terminal repair. A model receives an exact
connector graph with unavailable vertices, two named terminals, and
an image that determines a third terminal through geometry matching.
It must recover every minimum restoration set and decide whether a
given budget suffices. Neither the visual match nor the graph alone
determines the complete answer. A factorial construction independently
varies the visual binding and unavailable vertices, with matched
answer-preserving visual edits and independent public-ID replicates.
The released finite source collection yields \VRConstructions{}
constructions from \VRSources{} assemblies, grouped into \VRGroups{}
dependence groups, with \VRObservations{} repair observations.
Exhaustive certificates and an independent semantic scorer verify
binding, all optimal solutions, costs and component outcomes.
Atomic-binding and oracle-binding conditions separate the two
operations. Task-aligned joint outcomes measure multimodal failure
on observations solved in both isolated conditions; legal shallow
baselines expose avoidable shortcuts.
We release construction, exclusion and split ledgers, native inputs,
scoring and a versioned analysis path. Learned-model tables remain
blank pending collection. The contribution is a controlled test
of the binding--optimization interface on this finite resource,
not a claim of physical repair or population-wide generalization.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A vision--language model may identify the right object but fail to
reason about the system containing it. It may also solve a graph
problem perfectly when the relevant object is named, yet bind that
name to the wrong visual candidate. Ordinary task accuracy conflates
these failures. The useful question is therefore not merely whether
a model can recognize bricks or solve connectivity problems, but
whether the two operations remain correct when their interaction
changes under controlled conditions.

BrickAtlas makes this interaction explicit. A supplied undirected
graph describes recognized connections inside a real LDraw
subassembly. Some vertices are unavailable. Two terminal vertices
are named in text; a third is selected by matching an unlabeled
reference part to one of four image-labeled candidates.
The model must enumerate all smallest restoration sets connecting
the three terminals, report their costs and resulting component
sizes, and decide whether a stated budget suffices.
The geometry match determines \emph{which} graph problem to solve.
The fault state determines \emph{how} to solve it.

The task has two independently manipulated inputs.
A target--distractor label transposition changes the selected
terminal while preserving the textual graph and fault set.
A different fault assignment changes the optimization problem while
preserving the image. Both interventions must change the complete
optimal-solution set before a construction is admitted.
A distractor--distractor transposition uses the same visual edit
mechanism while preserving the answer. Every construction also
has independent public-ID assignments, so source numbering cannot
serve as an undeclared correspondence rule.

This design supports a specific diagnosis. The atomic condition
tests visual binding alone. The oracle condition names the correct
terminal and tests structural optimization. The multimodal condition
requires both, and the no-image condition retains the full graph
without the terminal binding. Pair and factorial scores then test
whether correct behavior survives required changes and irrelevant
edits. Joint outcomes distinguish failure on individually solved
instances from nonoverlapping isolated competence.
The task does not measure inference of hidden connectors
from pixels: providing the graph deliberately makes structural
evidence exact and the remaining visual dependency identifiable.

Our contribution is a source-backed benchmark of this interface,
with admissibility based on exact changes to solution sets; matched
controls and grouping at construction time; and semantic scoring
that accepts equivalent valid outputs while retaining every failure.
The repair objective, CAD assets, shape matching and paired accuracy
are established ideas. Their composition is tested here through a
fully auditable factorial contract. The benchmark does not assume
that learned models outperform shallow systems or that a factorial
score must change their ranking.

\section{Related Work}
Controlled VQA datasets including CLEVR and GQA connect visual
questions to explicit scene programs~\cite{johnson2017clevr,hudson2019gqa}.
VQA v2 and contrast sets reduce the value of stable answer
priors~\cite{goyal2017vqa,gardner2020contrast}; consistency tests
distinguish invariance from correctness~\cite{shah2019cycle}.
Winoground and its analysis emphasize that joint failure can mix
binding, visibility and off-construct
requirements~\cite{thrush2022winoground,diwan2022winoground}.
Our binding task is deliberately narrower than unrestricted
referring-expression understanding~\cite{mao2016refexp}.

VisualFLIP directly evaluates independently queried answer-changing
image pairs and competence-conditioned
failure~\cite{zhu2026visualflip}. BrickAtlas does not introduce these
metrics. Its primary operation is instead a visual-to-graph binding
followed by exact optimization, with a second independently varied
structural input and a complete solution-set certificate.
The distinction concerns inputs and permitted conclusions, not an
assertion that other benchmarks lack useful visual controls.

BrickNet provides graph-backed human-authored LDraw assembly
programs~\cite{kulits2026bricknet}. LEGO-Puzzles evaluates multistep
spatial reasoning~\cite{tang2025legopuzzles}, while PhyBlock studies
physical understanding and assembly
planning~\cite{ma2025phyblock}. Our graph restoration is an abstract
decision problem: a restored vertex recovers its original edges.
It does not model insertion, extraction, forces or tool use.

\section{Task and Construction}
\subsection{Public input and semantic output}
Let $G=(V,E)$ be the supplied graph, $M\subseteq V$ the unavailable
vertices, $t_1,t_2$ the explicit terminals, and $C\subseteq V$
the four candidate IDs. In image $I$, $\phi(I,C)$ is the ID of
the unique candidate matching the reference geometry, ignoring
color and pose. Public IDs are arbitrary references; original
instance identities remain private provenance.
For a restoration $R\subseteq M$, write
\[
G_R=G[V\setminus(M\setminus R)],\quad
T=\{t_1,t_2,\phi(I,C)\}.
\]
Every restoration costs one per vertex. A feasible $R$ places
all vertices in $T$ in one component of $G_R$.
Define
\begin{align*}
k^\star&=\min_{R\subseteq M:\,T\text{ connected in }G_R}|R|,\\
\mathcal{R}^\star&=\{R: R\text{ feasible},\,|R|=k^\star\}.
\end{align*}
The output includes $\phi$, $k^\star$, the budget decision
$[k^\star\leq b]$, every $R\in\mathcal R^\star$, and the multiset
of component sizes for each corresponding $G_R$.
Order of sets and IDs has no semantic meaning.
This richer output prevents credit for one feasible but nonminimal
repair or an incomplete list of equally optimal repairs.

The native packet includes the complete question, graph, unavailable
IDs, two explicit terminals, candidate IDs, budget and output schema.
Only the image supplies the binding. Source names, original IDs,
part numbers, coordinates, intervention labels and GT are excluded.
The exact per-condition text and PNG hashes define the scored input;
human-readable manifests additionally retain grouping metadata.

\subsection{Factorial controls and dependency certificates}
Each construction has two fault states differing by one
unavailable/present exchange, three visual arms, and two independent
public-ID replicates. The visual arms are anchor, a matching-candidate
label transposition, and a two-distractor label transposition.
Thus a construction has twelve repair observations, which remain
one dependent unit.

For each fault state, the visual-changing pair must have different
complete optimal-solution sets. For the fixed visual binding, the
two fault states must also change those sets. The visual-preserving
pair must have identical sets, costs and component outputs.
All certificates are computed before model evaluation.
These are requirements on \emph{semantic} solutions after decoding
public IDs, not on changes in JSON order.

Each candidate card uses actual LDraw geometry at its saved part
pose; the reference has a different camera view.
Cards are object observations, not proposed freestanding assemblies.
Color and numeric ID are not matching criteria. The unedited source
assembly is available in the inspection interface to establish
provenance; it does not enter the primary request.

\subsection{Sampling, exclusions and held-out constructions}
The target population is the finite checked-in source collection.
We enumerate complete non-root subassemblies, including descendants,
with 12--100 parts, a connected recognized-connector graph and full
recorded connector coverage. Subassemblies with a recorded unmatched
intersection inside the induced set are excluded.
This rule is a dataset screen, not a physical certificate.

A seeded ordering selects disjoint instance sets within each source.
Aligned typed origin-distance signatures detect repeated designs.
Conservative graph-refinement signatures group potential repeated
topological templates. Sources linked by contributors, design
signatures or topology signatures form transitive dependence groups.
Development and held-out groups are assigned before bounded
structural search. All views, fault variants and ID replicates of
a construction remain together. The ledger retains candidates,
exclusions, search attempts and the first admissible result; failed
search does not silently disappear from the sampling account.

\subsection{Shallow controls and qualification}
Frozen legal baselines receive only their declared public inputs.
These include fixed/sorted-ID rules, graph-based repair heuristics,
card-location choices, and pixel silhouette/appearance matching.
A binding predictor followed by exhaustive graph search is reported
as a modular baseline; it is not learned end-to-end reasoning.
Oracle binding is explicitly privileged. Strong shallow performance
requires a narrower interpretation, not removal of those tasks after
inspection. The declared threshold is .80 held-out binding or exact
repair micro accuracy for any legal shallow baseline.
The actual silhouette-binding plus exact-graph solver reaches
\VRPixelMicro{}\% held-out exact repair
(\VRPixelMacro{}\% equal-group; \VRHeldoutN{} observations,
\VRHeldoutGroups{} groups), activating this rule.
Oracle binding plus exact search reaches 100\%.
The binding stage is therefore shallow-solvable; the benchmark
tests reliable composition with that stage, not hard visual
recognition. The full algorithm audit remains in the supplement.

Human qualification judges native inputs for reference uniqueness,
candidate visibility and unique visual matching, separately from the
exact graph certificate. Six reviewer slots assign two people to
each visual arm; repeated IDs/faults within an arm are dependent
judgments, and reviewers never cross arms of that construction.
Ambiguous or conflicting judgments require independent adjudication and requalification after
stimulus revision. Full planned denominators remain available
alongside a qualified subset, with counts and exclusions.
Human evidence remains uncollected in the present workspace.

\section{Evaluation Protocol}
\subsection{Semantic correctness and failure handling}
The independent scorer uses union-find connectivity rather than the
constructor's graph search and enumerates all candidate restorations.
It validates IDs, duplicates, terminal binding, minimum cost, budget
decision, complete optimal-set coverage and component results.
Alternative valid orderings receive identical credit. Malformed JSON,
unknown vertices, omitted optima, extra nonoptimal solutions and
inconsistent metadata fail the appropriate criterion. Test fixtures
are labeled synthetic and cannot become empirical receipts.

Let $c_{rsav}\in\{0,1\}$ indicate exact correctness for construction
$r$, ID replicate $s$, structural arm $a$ and visual arm $v$.
Exact correctness requires binding \emph{and} complete semantic
repair. Binding accuracy is also reported independently.
The three paired metrics require both members correct: anchor versus
visual change, anchor versus visual preservation, and fault zero
versus fault one. Factorial correctness is
\[
F_{rs}=\prod_{a\in\{0,1\}}\prod_{v\in\{A,C,P\}}c_{rsav}.
\]
A constant wrong answer never earns preserving correctness.
Conditional repair given correct binding is undefined with zero
eligible observations; its denominator accompanies the score.

\subsection{Conditions and estimands}
The primary multimodal condition solves binding and repair jointly.
Atomic binding uses the same image and candidates but omits the
graph and fault state. Oracle binding names the true terminal and
omits the image. No-image repair includes the graph and candidates
without the binding. These conditions distinguish a missing visual
dependency from imperfect optimization given a correct binding.
They do not prove any particular internal reasoning procedure.

For the same task, ID replicate and intervention cell $i$, let
$A_i$ denote atomic binding correctness, $O_i$ oracle exact repair,
$M_i$ multimodal exact repair, and $B_i$ multimodal binding correctness.
We retain all eight $(A_i,O_i,M_i)$ contingency counts and define
\[
J_{\rm micro}=
\frac{\sum_i A_iO_i(1-M_i)}{\sum_i A_iO_i}.
\]
This is observed joint-condition failure among observations solved
in both isolated conditions. It is not a causal measure of an
internal reasoning mechanism. A zero denominator is undefined.
The primary equal-group $J$ averages the within-group ratios over
groups with at least one jointly competent observation; the
eligible observation and group counts accompany it.
We also report paired differences $M_i-O_i$ in exact repair and
$B_i-A_i$ in binding. Marginal accuracies cannot recover $J$.

Joins require the same locked model revision, adapter, settings,
task membership and qualification eligibility across conditions.
Missing or invalid planned calls contribute zero correctness;
an unplanned condition makes its contrast unavailable.
Micro estimates weight observations; group estimates weight
dependence groups equally. Shared-group resampling retains all
conditions, images, anchors and ID replicates together.
The supplement freezes zero-eligibility and resampling details.
Full-set and construction-wide qualified-set outputs retain
separate denominators, with pending and rejected counts.
Group counts and leave-one-group-out sensitivity accompany results.
These are descriptive finite-resource analyses. A worst-case normal
half-width target of .20 for independent bounded group means would
already require 25 groups before small-sample corrections. More
renderings cannot remedy fewer independent groups.

The versioned study manifest binds exact condition inputs, evaluator
version, planned slots and baseline configurations.
An external run lock must additionally pin provider/model revision,
adapter, image handling, output settings and failure policy.
Only receipts matching the selected version and input hashes are
eligible; a missing slot remains incorrect. A provider-neutral
interface does not claim that an unpinned proprietary endpoint is
already operational. There are no paid-model results in this release.

\section{Planned Experiments}
The study asks whether binding-only and oracle-repair accuracy
predict success when both are required, whether that success
survives the visual and structural interventions, and how much
legal shallow or modular systems explain. Null effects and strong
baselines are valid outcomes.

conditions, not the image-binding condition. Identifiers come from

Unsupported effort-mode comparisons from earlier plans are not

The primary result matrix (Table~\ref{tab:primary}) is interpreted
together with the atomic, no-image and oracle conditions, reported
with the joint-condition matrix in the supplement.
A low multimodal score despite strong oracle
performance is compatible with binding failure; it is not by itself
proof of failed structural reasoning. Likewise, high atomic binding
with low oracle repair locates a structural limitation without
requiring a visual explanation. Factorial failures can arise at
either stage and must be decomposed accordingly.
In particular, identical condition accuracies can conceal opposite
joint outcomes: $J$ asks whether isolated successes occur on the
same observations as multimodal failures. Zero $J$, either sign
of a paired difference, and no eligible observations are all
permitted outcomes.

All response slots and revisions must be frozen before real
collection. The current release provides deterministic construction
and baseline audits plus executable scoring; planned model and human
results are not represented by fabricated values. Historical Color,
Part-type and small graph studies remain separately versioned and
are not pooled with the new repair family.

\section{Limitations}
BrickAtlas isolates one visual-to-symbolic interface. It does not
recover connectivity from images, model the feasibility of inserting
a part, certify stable physical assemblies, or evaluate unrestricted
repair planning. Candidate cards make the binding problem explicit
and may favor shallow appearance matching; such performance narrows
what end-to-end errors can establish.
Recognized connectors may be incomplete despite exact graph
evaluation. Geometry equality does not guarantee human-visible
uniqueness, making native-input qualification necessary.

The finite collection has dependent sources, repeated part types and
limited construction groups. Grouping is conservative, not proof
that all latent similarities were found. Held-out group performance
supports claims within the declared construction population only.
Selection requires informative counterfactuals, so the benchmark
does not estimate natural failure frequency.
