\begin{abstract}
Assembly agents must maintain and repair correct work while progressing
toward a goal. We introduce \ours, a benchmark connecting
static structure repair with continuous assembly under hidden interventions.
Its 5,452 main candidates cover local structures, complete objects, and
identifiable remaining modules. Seven physical error labels are separated
from access-related rework and repeated incidents. Agents inspect RGB
states and manipulate one brick at a time; terminal scoring accepts
interchangeable parts and alternative legal construction orders. We define
correction, damage, and recovery metrics with explicit exposure and
control requirements, preventing an unreached fault or failed API call
from becoming a visual-miss claim. Reference replay and route diagnostics validate execution; formal
learned-agent and independent human results remain unmeasured.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
An agent assembling a car prepares its roof, but an earlier piece is
removed before the next observation. The target remains unchanged;
completed work does not. Finishing requires relating the goal, action
history, and actual scene, then executing a legal repair. Choosing the
next step on an undisturbed manual does not test this ability.

Such a task also exposes weaknesses in evaluation. A car can be assembled
in several legal orders. Comparing each intermediate state with a single
demonstration rejects legitimate progress. Scheduling a fault only when
the agent reproduces that demonstration's prefix can prevent a valid
alternative route from ever encountering the fault. Conversely, a model
that cannot build normally provides little evidence about repair under
intervention. Completion, physical legality, exposure, and recovery need
different definitions and denominators.

Visual specification also matters. A finished-object image can hide
joints; an arbitrary crop can lack identifiable purpose. \ours separates
local static diagnostics from dynamic object construction. Small original
LDraw files retain every part; large scenes supply a recognizable body
and a named remaining module. One target defines acceptance; available
manuals explain structure without prescribing its only valid sequence.

Prior work establishes buildable brick generation~\cite{pun2025brickgpt,kulits2026bricknet},
manual execution~\cite{wang2022mepnet}, step correction~\cite{wan2024scanet},
and interactive reconstruction~\cite{walsman2022breakmake}. Visual-spatial
benchmarks further show the value of separating task families and
reasoning demands~\cite{yang2025thinking,wang2025spatial457}.
Our focus is continuous error recovery under a controlled visual action
contract: the world can change after the agent has already made progress,
and the agent is not interrupted to answer an anomaly questionnaire.

We contribute: (1) a source-organized collection of 5,720 distinct task
records, with 5,452 main candidates and separately retained diagnostics;
(2) a shared RGB/action contract that accepts physical endpoint equivalence
and legal alternative orders; and (3) metrics distinguishing static
correction from damage and dynamic recovery from exposure, with reference
checks and reproducible protocol diagnostics. The current evidence
establishes executor properties. The planned agent study will test
perception and planning under this contract.

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Generation, execution, and reconstruction.}
BrickGPT generates buildable structures with validity checks and
rollback~\cite{pun2025brickgpt}. BrickNet represents diverse LDraw parts
through connection graphs and executable sequences~\cite{kulits2026bricknet};
BrickBench studies agentic brick design~\cite{kulits2026brickbench}.
Our intended structure is supplied: the objective is restoration or
completion rather than open-ended design quality.

MEPNet translates visual manuals into executable plans~\cite{wang2022mepnet}.
LEGO-ECA and SCANet study errors associated with assembly
steps~\cite{wan2024scanet}. Break and Make combines interactive inspection,
disassembly, and reconstruction~\cite{walsman2022breakmake}.
Our static condition connects to correction; our dynamic condition
retains the ongoing agent history across an external change.

\paragraph{Spatial reasoning and adaptation.}
LEGO-Puzzles and PhyBlock study spatial understanding and block
planning~\cite{tang2025legopuzzles,ma2025phyblock}. VSI-Bench evaluates
spatial recall from video~\cite{yang2025thinking}, while Spatial457
organizes visual questions by spatial capabilities~\cite{wang2025spatial457}.
AsgardBench and RoboRecover address interactive adaptation and
recovery~\cite{tupini2026asgard,li2026roborecover}. Thus neither spatial
reasoning nor recovery itself is new. Our setting combines a specified
brick target, one-part physical actions, alternative-order acceptance,
and qualified comparison of control and intervened continuations.

\section{The BrickPlan Collection}
\subsection{Task scopes and construction}
Table~\ref{tab:dataset} summarizes the main scopes. Static repair starts
from a supplied state and ends at a specified stage. A local structure
can be useful even when it is not a recognizable complete object.
Dynamic tasks instead require a complete small object or an identifiable
remaining module. Both retain a fixed coordinate frame and white-baseplate
environment; module tasks additionally supply an immutable surrounding body.

The dynamic collection has 63 operation configurations. Fifty are whole
objects: all 42 earlier procedural designs, five longer procedural objects,
and three complete original LDraw files. The originals are a ship (4018,
12 parts), sea helicopter (4017, 13), and mini helicopter (4037, nine).
Their source instances, colors, and poses are retained without dropping
parts. This denotes source-file completeness, not retail bill-of-materials
verification. Longer builds include a bookshelf, bus, bridge, gantry press,
and cottage with 21--32 active pieces.

Thirteen module configurations come from twelve community scenes, plus
an enlarged museum roof on an existing source. Their supplied bodies
make the purpose of the remaining work explicit. The original modules
have 5--12 active pieces; the enlarged roof has sixteen. A large source
scene therefore does not imply a large action task. All earlier chairs,
tables, cars, and mechanisms retain their provenance; the supplement
illustrates every main dynamic configuration and lists all 160 original
static configurations.

\subsection{Physical errors and process demands}
Figure~\ref{fig:errors} shows seven basic physical labels: missing,
extra, wrong color, wrong part, position/height offset, non-equivalent
orientation, and positional swap. Wrong-part labels include size and
thickness. Extra-layer examples add local pieces; they do not duplicate
an entire object layer. Labels may co-occur, so a compound state is not
forced into one exclusive class.

Process tags capture a different burden. Premature covering can obstruct
later insertion; buried repair can require temporarily removing correct
work. A missing repeated element still carries the missing-piece label.
Figure~\ref{fig:access} shows why repair complexity is not exhausted by
counting defective parts. Several simultaneous defects also differ from
separate accidents over time: the main set has 37 two-event and six
three-event conditions.

Twelve approved human-inspired source tags motivate these synthetic
conditions. They are analogues, not a dataset of observed human mistake
trajectories or estimates of human error frequencies. Excluded and
deferred categories remain documented in the release specification.

\subsection{Structural descriptors and source grouping}
We record build length B1 ($\leq12$ active target pieces), B2 (13--24),
and B3 ($\geq25$). Static B counts the acceptance stage, not future
manual pieces or supplied scenery. Rework R0 denotes controls; R1
requires at most two reference moves without correct-cover removal;
R3 includes such removal or at least seven reference moves; R2 contains
the remaining ordinary/multiple-defect rework. These are witness burdens,
not proven minimum costs. T0--T3 count external events; static errors
already exist at the start and are T0.

Provisional grades combine the descriptors: G3 means B3, T3, or repeated
events with R3; otherwise B2, R2/R3, or T2 gives G2; the remainder is G1.
Their main counts are 2,577/2,823/52. They are planning strata rather
than empirically calibrated easy/medium/hard labels. Visibility remains
an independent admission field.

Original sets/procedural families define 154 disjoint source groups;
45 displayed/debugged sources are public development. The existing-source
test has 392 main candidates (309 local static, 83 whole dynamic).
Community modules and original-object examples are currently public
development; new-source claims require untouched sources.

\section{Interactive Task and Execution}
\subsection{A unique target, RGB observations, and one action}
A task specifies fixed context $B$, movable parts $P$, registered poses
$C_p$, initial state $s_0$, and acceptance target $g$.
At each decision the agent sees the actual current RGB state and can
inspect supported isometric, rear, or top views. Previous observations
provide temporal evidence. A static target is its required stage; a
dynamic target is the completed object/module. Future manual pages
remain references and never silently replace the acceptance target.

Current/target panels share framing ($800\times600$ each on a
$1600\times636$ canvas). Modules retain full-object context. Supported
tracks offer on-demand manuals; legacy local static does not. RGB,
neutral rules, and anonymous handles are public. Inventory lists spares,
not live remaining counts; geometric state stays private.

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures-publication/interaction.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Concrete observation--action--execution loop.} An example static repair removes the wrong-color brick. The resulting state is legal but incomplete, so the next decision must use the new observation. Each response is one direct action object; inspections remain available throughout. States are re-rendered here for print; agent inputs use the native RGB canvases.}
\label{fig:interaction}
\end{figure*}

The shared response is a single direct action object:
\begin{equation}
 a_t\in\{\operatorname{attach}(p,c),\operatorname{detach}(p),
          \operatorname{inspect}(q),\operatorname{finish}\}.
\end{equation}
A manipulation moves exactly one brick. Inspection can query a view,
part, candidate preview, inventory page, or supported manual step.
Previews are hypothetical; they neither execute a placement nor certify
it as correct. The current input version uses the same response envelope
across tracks. Free-coordinate prediction and explicit text-state assistance
are separately planned experiments with different information/action burdens.

\subsection{Legal transitions and endpoint equivalence}
Attachment checks a supported compatible connection, endpoint collision,
and straight insertion path; detachment checks extraction. A rejected
attempt leaves geometry unchanged. Legality feedback can aid planning,
but a legal placement may still be semantically wrong. Subsequent correction
can still yield success. Finite poses and rigid geometry do not model
force, slipping, articulated motion, or a gripper.

Let $\sig(s)$ be the multiset of installed part shape, color, and pose,
using only supported symmetry equivalences. Completion is
\begin{equation}
 C(\tau)=\mathbf{1}[a_T=\operatorname{finish}]
          \mathbf{1}[\sig(s_T)=\sig(g)].
 \label{eq:success}
\end{equation}
This accepts interchangeable pieces and alternative legal orders,
with only justified part symmetries and no arbitrary global alignment.
Immutable context is excluded from scores.

\subsection{Intervening on actual progress}
Hidden edits are applied after accepted manipulations and before the
next observation. The private log retains their timing, legal primitives,
and realized effect. Adaptive module selectors search the actual installed
state; older conditions can have template-dependent opportunities.
An infeasible edit is deferred or remains untriggered. A scheduled event
or nominal step threshold is not evidence of exposure.

Multiple incidents each receive a separate record. Removing a just-added
piece can cancel the latest action and leave before/after images alike;
this action-outcome case is flagged separately from a change to older
work. An edit that removes an already-wrong placement may help the agent.
Premature covering can block future access even when the cover belongs
to the final goal. These cases require effect checks, rather than judging
all incidents solely by added final-state residual.

\section{Evaluation: Correction, Exposure, and Recovery}
\subsection{Static repair without collateral damage}
S0 denotes already-correct acceptance (260 tasks); S1 denotes repair
(3,437). Let $I,F,G$ be initial, final, and target movable-placement
multisets and $H=I\cap G$ the initially correct work. Exact repair SR
averages Eq.~\ref{eq:success} over valid S1 runs. Partial quality,
error reduction, and final damage are
\begin{align}
 \mathrm{F1}_{\rm place}&=\frac{2|F\cap G|}{|F|+|G|},\\
 d(A,G)&=|A-G|+|G-A|,\\
 \mathrm{ER}&=1-\frac{d(F,G)}{d(I,G)},\qquad
 \mathrm{DR}=\frac{|H-F|}{|H|}.
\end{align}
ER can be negative. Residual counts physical mismatches, not human
mistakes or proven shortest repair actions. Temporarily removing and
restoring a correct cover does not create final DR. On S0, false repair
FR records any accepted attach/detach, including remove-and-restore;
final acceptance is also reported. S0 and S1 are not pooled.
Undefined denominators are N/A; F1 of two empty sets is one.

Figure~\ref{fig:metric} illustrates why SR and partial quality are both
needed. Supplemental scores track restoration of original missing
placements, clearance of original excess, and attribute-combination F1.
A connection-adjacency F1 is planned but is not yet uniformly implemented.

\subsection{Dynamic completion needs qualified exposure}
D0 measures normal construction (63 tasks); D1 assigns one incident
(1,649); D2 assigns repeated incidents (43). BCR is valid D0 completion.
ICR is raw completion of valid intervention-assigned runs, including
untriggered ones. TR counts applied events over planned events. Neither
raw completion nor trigger coverage establishes visual detection.

A paired study executes a common prefix once, saves the environment,
conversation, images, mapping, and used budget, then branches into normal
and fault continuations (Fig.~\ref{fig:paired}). Subsequent legal orders
can differ. Define $Q$ as pairs with valid branches, a verified shared
prefix, all scheduled events applied, and verified incident effects.
For normal/fault success $c_i,f_i$, report
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{CRR}=\frac{\sum_{i\in Q}c_i f_i}{\sum_{i\in Q}c_i},\qquad
 \Delta\mathrm{SR}=\frac{1}{|Q|}\sum_{i\in Q}(c_i-f_i).
\end{equation}
CRR additionally conditions on successful normal construction; the signed
paired difference retains normal failures. Report planned/valid pairs,
failed controls, untriggered/partial exposure, unverified effects, and
eligible $N$. Two fresh runs sharing a nominal seed are not proven
shared-prefix pairs. An unreached fault is not a missed detection.

\subsection{Repair while making new progress}
For an event with states $B$ before and $A$ after, define lost correct
placements $L=(B\cap G)-A$ and incremental excess
$X=(A-G)-(B-G)$. Recovery restores these quantities, without requiring
the later state to equal $B$. Additional correct construction is allowed.
Each event window ends before the next external intervention.

Report first-recovery decisions, window-end recovery, and retention over
at least two follow-up decisions. Inspections and invalid actions count
as decisions. Observed recurrence fails retention; a correct but too-short
window is N/A. A pure access obstruction with empty $L,X$ receives no
automatic zero latency. All-event D2 recovery requires complete effective
exposure and final success; partial exposure remains a separate outcome.
Repair@2/4/8 is reserved for sufficiently observed single-event/checkpoint
windows. A future checkpoint C bank supplies the prefix explicitly and
will not count that prefix as model-built progress.

\subsection{Validity, cost, and aggregation}
Wrong finish and model budget exhaustion are ability failures. Final
service errors, harness errors, and external stops are censored from
ability rates and reported separately. A recovered same-decision retry
can produce a valid run, while its charge remains. Parsing, schema/handle
errors, and physical rejections have separate counters. An incorrect
episode alone cannot establish a visual detection miss; independent
snapshot detection/localization remains a separate planned task.

Normalize canonical-task weights, average within objects, and report
object- and source-macro estimates alongside task-micro references.
Keep input version, scope, and task type separate. Report valid coverage
and completion per planned attempt so access failures cannot disappear.
Costs include inspections, all attempts, images, tokens, shared prefixes,
both continuations, and billed retries. Reference-cost ratios use feasible
witnesses and can be below one; they are not optimality or SPL. Future
source-clustered intervals retain variants together. One run per condition
does not establish repeatability.

Old cropped-module checks do not certify the new full-context interface.
Complete image/human qualification remains outstanding.

\subsection{Does reference-order scheduling change the test?}
A selected-route diagnostic uses twelve community modules, two legal
routes, and four conditions per route: 96 trials. Alternative routes
were chosen as regression counterexamples, not sampled from a model.
All 24 normal routes reach the same targets. Requiring every manual
prefix accepts zero of twelve alternatives, demonstrating why terminal
physical equivalence is necessary.

\paragraph{Admission and interpretation.}
Predeclare admitted task IDs and inspect their native images, inventory,
and queries. Retain ambiguous cases and reasons. Adaptive-event visibility
requires checking the realized location. Cost pilots record actual images,
resizing, and tokens per decision; conceptual view count does not establish
constant image cost.

A planned timing analysis bins incidents by physical progress
$|\sig(s)\cap\sig(g)|/|\sig(g)|$, preserving alternative orders.
Report progress alongside B/R/T. Separate accepted moves, rejections,
queries, and residual improvement before attributing errors to perception
or reasoning; endpoint recovery alone does not identify its internal cause.

The planned graphical human panel has 72 conditions, six participants,
and three independent runs per condition, without prose or JSON. Nobody sees
both pair members. Continuous trials report completion and exposure;
independent participants are not cloned-history pairs. System-supplied
prefixes are C tasks, and human causal rates need their own pairing design. Nineteen
unmeasured result sheets in the supplement preserve static, dynamic,
paired, repeated-event, input/output, detection, human, and cost analyses.
Checkpoint, independent-detection, and alternative-state/coordinate banks
remain to be built or integrated. No formal performance or cost estimate
is inferred from these plans.

\paragraph{Scope of current evidence.}
Full-context visual admission, independent human study, paired API
logging, and source-bootstrap reporting remain pending. Complete original
objects, long community modules, and untouched sources are limited;
public files may occur in pretraining. Finite candidates, fixed context,
conservative obstacles, and straight paths simplify manipulation. Articulation,
flexibility, force stability, and robot control are outside scope.

\section{Conclusion}
\ours connects static correction with continuous assembly through a
shared visual action contract. It accepts legal alternative routes,
separates physical discrepancies from access/repetition demands, and
makes exposure and control eligibility explicit. The current collection,
metrics, and executor diagnostics provide a reproducible basis for the
remaining learned-agent and independent human study.
